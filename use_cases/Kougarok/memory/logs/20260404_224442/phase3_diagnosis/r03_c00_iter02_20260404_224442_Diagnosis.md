## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- ECA competition zero-sum conflict: PFT9 (deciduous shrub) with stoich_phos_leaf_9 at upper bound (0.00428) in Case #86 has P demand of 39,944 g/m²/yr, which starves PFT10 (graminoid, demand 16,576 g/m²/yr) via ECA competition. The ECA framework allocates P proportionally to root biomass × Vmax, but with total demand 126,187× supply, even PFT7 (which takes 63.2% of actual uptake) achieves only 0.0001 supply/demand ratio. Reducing stoich_phos_leaf_9 directly frees competitive pressure on PFT10.
- Shared allometry parameter constraints: allom_d2h2_7 and allom_d2h2_9 are both at upper bounds in Case #86 (0.521 and 0.530 respectively), creating tall-for-diameter shrub morphologies that may shade PFT10 graminoids. However, with PFT10 at near-zero biomass, light competition is secondary — P starvation is collapsing PFT10 before it can reach the canopy to compete for light.
- PID controller cross-PFT feedback: The PID controller for each PFT independently drives fine root allocation in response to nutrient stress. With all three PFTs P-limited simultaneously, the combined fine root growth increases overall P demand further (more root biomass × vmax = higher ECA demand), creating a system-wide positive feedback that accelerates labile P depletion. This feedback loop cannot be broken by any single PFT parameter change — it requires the protocol-level fix (TRANS_SUPLPHOS).
- Shared maintenance respiration (maintresp_nonleaf_baserate=2.31e-06 in Case #86): This shared parameter affects all three PFTs' carbon balance. At the lower value within the ensemble range, respiration costs are reduced, marginally helping PFT10's carbon starvation mortality (27% of PFT10 deaths). Not a primary driver but interacts with PFT10's collapsed leaf area for carbon assimilation.
