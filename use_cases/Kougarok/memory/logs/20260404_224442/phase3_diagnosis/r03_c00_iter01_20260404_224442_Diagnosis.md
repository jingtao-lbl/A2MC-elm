Comprehensive diagnosis of the Kougarok Phase 2 screening failure across 4890 cases. The dominant finding is a universal, catastrophic phosphorus limitation affecting all three PFTs (7, 9, 10) simultaneously. The diagnostic data provides unambiguous evidence: (1) all three PFTs show P uptake/demand ratios of 0.00, (2) labile P pool is 100% depleted (13.90 → 0.00 g/m²), (3) total P demand across PFTs is 126,187 g/m²/yr while total supply is only 1.51 g/m²/yr — a 4-orders-of-magnitude mismatch that cannot be corrected by any parameter adjustment within the current ensemble. The current spinup protocol uses SUPLPHOS=NONE during transition, meaning plants enter the transient phase without adequate P reserves. This combination creates a positive feedback: P starvation → PID controller reallocates C to fine roots → fine roots take more P but demand is so extreme that uptake fraction remains ~0 → biomass collapses. PFT10 (graminoid) is most severely affected (leaf: -98.6%, froot: -97.8%), followed by PFT9 (leaf: -52.1%) and PFT7 (leaf: -22.4%). The parameter bounds analysis for Case #86 confirms 39 parameters at bounds (24.1% of 162), with P-relevant parameters especially constrained (vmax_nh4_7, vmax_nh4_9, km_nh4_7, km_no3_7, km_ptase_7, stoich_phos_leaf_9, stoich_phos_fineroot_9, stoich_phos_fineroot_7, stoich_phos_leaf_10). The test_morris_bounds_impact diagnostic confirms this with 95% confidence. Critically, the demand figure (mean 870,842 gP/m²/yr for PFT7 alone) is physically impossible and suggests either a unit conversion error in the demand calculation or that the ECA uptake kinetics are receiving an unbounded demand signal — the latter is consistent with near-zero soil P availability driving unbounded Michaelis-Menten demand. Causal chain: LABILEP depleted → soil P near-zero → ECA uptake approaches 0 → plant P demand unsatisfied → PID controller drives extreme fine root allocation → fine root turnover flux increases → more P locked in litter → LABILEP further depleted (positive feedback). The simulation protocol (TRANS_SUPLPHOS=NONE) means this runaway depletion is not corrected during calibration runs.

## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (vmax_p_7, vmax_p_9, vmax_p_10) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** increase upper bound by 10-100x (e.g., to 5e-04 for vmax_p, 2.5e-03 for vmax_nh4)
- **Issue:** P uptake demand-supply ratio = 0.00 for all PFTs. Current upper bound (5e-05) may be insufficient to achieve meaningful P uptake given near-zero soil P. The constraint test_morris_bounds_impact confirmed 6 P-uptake parameters at bounds with 95% confidence.

### fates_cnp_eca_km_p (km_p_7, km_p_9, km_p_10) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** decrease lower bound to 0.01-0.02 (arctic-adapted low-Km uptake systems)
- **Issue:** With near-zero soil P (LABILEP depleted), reducing Km would allow plants to take up P at lower concentrations. Current range [0.05, 0.15] may be too high for P-depleted arctic soils.

### fates_cnp_eca_alpha_ptase (alpha_ptase_10) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** Hold at high values but expand range to 1.0 if mechanistically justified; prioritize soil P replenishment instead
- **Issue:** PFT10 alpha_ptase at upper bound (0.95 in Case #86). Phosphatase efficiency is already maximized for graminoid but graminoid biomass is still collapsing, suggesting phosphatase alone cannot compensate for labile P depletion.

### fates_stoich_phos (stoich_phos_leaf_7, stoich_phos_leaf_9, stoich_phos_leaf_10) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** For PFT9: decrease stoich_phos_leaf_9 upper bound or use lower values. For PFT10: current lower bound (0.000920964) may already be appropriate but needs more range below
- **Issue:** stoich_phos_leaf_9 and stoich_phos_fineroot_9 are at upper bounds; stoich_phos_leaf_10 and stoich_phos_fineroot_7 are at lower bounds. High target P:C ratios increase P demand when plants already cannot meet current demand. Case #86 uses stoich_phos_leaf_9=0.00428 (upper bound).

### fates_cnp_turnover_phos_retrans (phos_retrans_7, phos_retrans_9, phos_retrans_10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** For phos_retrans_7: explore whether lower values (<0.6) help or hurt — mechanistically, higher retranslocation (0.8-0.9) should help P recycling under depletion
- **Issue:** phos_retrans_7 is at lower bound (0.6). Higher retranslocation means less P lost per leaf turnover cycle, critical when soil P is depleted. Current lower bound prevents exploring lower retranslocation efficiency.

### fates_allom_l2fr (l2fr_ini_7, l2fr_ini_9, l2fr_ini_10) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** Reduce upper bound for l2fr_ini_9 and l2fr_ini_7; allow lower values that don't force extreme root growth when P is depleted
- **Issue:** With P starvation, PID controller likely drives extreme fine root allocation (high L2FR for root exploration), depleting carbon from leaf pools. l2fr_ini_9 in Case #86 is 13.08 (very high), consistent with nutrient-stress-driven root proliferation.

### fates_turnover_leaf (turnover_leaf_7, turnover_leaf_10) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** Increase lower bound for turnover_leaf_10 (allow >0.3 yr to reduce P demand frequency). For turnover_leaf_7, expand upper bound if longer-lived evergreen leaves are supported by literature.
- **Issue:** turnover_leaf_7 is at upper bound (2.0 yr) and turnover_leaf_10 is at lower bound (0.3 yr). For PFT10 graminoids, very fast leaf turnover (0.3 yr) combined with P limitation means constant P demand for leaf rebuilding cannot be met.

## Protocol Recommendations

### A2MC_TRANS_SUPLPHOS (Priority: 1)
- **Current:** `NONE` → **Proposed:** `ALL`
- **Rationale:** With LABILEP fully depleted at end of spinup and P demand exceeding supply by 4 orders of magnitude, the transient calibration phase starts in a P-crisis state. Enabling P supplementation during transient phase allows vegetation to achieve biomass targets without being immediately P-starved. This is the most critical protocol change needed — without it, no parameter combination can achieve calibration targets.
- **Requires re-spinup:** No

### A2MC_RGSP_SUPLPHOS (Priority: 2)
- **Current:** `ALL` → **Proposed:** `ALL`
- **Rationale:** Keep P supplementation during regular spinup. However, verify that the spinup duration is sufficient to equilibrate P pools before removing supplementation. The 100% LABILEP depletion suggests either the spinup is not equilibrating properly, or the P cycling parameters are creating a drain faster than inputs can replenish.
- **Requires re-spinup:** No

### A2MC_ADSP_NYEARS_AD_CARBON_ONLY (Priority: 3)
- **Current:** `30` → **Proposed:** `50`
- **Rationale:** Extending carbon-only phase during AD spinup allows allometric structure to equilibrate before P cycling is activated. If plants enter P cycling with non-equilibrium biomass, the initial P demand surge can deplete soil P pools catastrophically. A 50-year carbon-only phase would provide more stable initial conditions for P cycling onset.
- **Requires re-spinup:** Yes

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- PFT7 vs PFT9 P competition: With near-zero soil P, PFT7 (evergreen shrub) takes 63.2% of total P uptake vs PFT9 (deciduous shrub) at 5.5%. This asymmetric P allocation starves PFT9 and PFT10, making it impossible to simultaneously achieve PFT7 leaf/root targets AND PFT9/PFT10 targets under current ECA competition dynamics.
- Shared soil P pool exhaustion: All three PFTs compete for the same depleted labile P pool. Increasing vmax for one PFT will further reduce availability for others unless soil P inputs (weathering, desorption) are also addressed.
- stoich_phos parameters push in opposite directions: stoich_phos_leaf_9/froot_9 at upper bounds (high P demand) vs stoich_phos_leaf_10/froot_7 at lower bounds (lower P demand). This P stoichiometry divergence across PFTs creates internally inconsistent optimization pressure.
- phen_mindaysoff interaction: PFT7 mindaysoff at 107 days (near default) vs PFT9/PFT10 at 79 days. Shorter dormancy for PFT9/10 means more P demand days per year, exacerbating P depletion for already-limited PFTs.
- alloc_storage_cushion_9 at upper bound (5.0) combined with alloc_store_priority_frac_9 at lower bound (0.406): PFT9 is configured to maintain a large carbon storage cushion but with low priority for filling it, creating allocation confusion under nutrient stress where the PID controller gets conflicting signals.
