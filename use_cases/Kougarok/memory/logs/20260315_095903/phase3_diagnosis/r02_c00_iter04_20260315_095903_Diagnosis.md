Case #3972 uses km_nh4_10=0.07 (lower bound) and achieves PFT10_leaf=21.1.

## Protocol Recommendations

### A2MC_RGSP_SUPLPHOS (Priority: 1)
- **Current:** `NONE` → **Proposed:** `ALL`
- **Rationale:** The P mass balance reveals total P input = 0.857 g/m2/yr vs plant P demand = 358,121 g/m2/yr (supply/demand = 1.9e-6). This 5-order-of-magnitude gap is a structural protocol problem: plants cannot accumulate realistic biomass during RGSP (Reduced Generic Spinup Phase) without P supplementation. Enabling SUPLPHOS=ALL during RGSP would provide unlimited P during the spinup phase, allowing vegetation to reach realistic biomass equilibrium based on carbon and N constraints alone. P limitation would then be applied progressively in TRANS (or at the start of transient simulations).
- **Requires re-spinup:** Yes

### A2MC_TRANS_SUPLPHOS (Priority: 2)
- **Current:** `NONE` → **Proposed:** `ALL`
- **Rationale:** If RGSP is fixed but TRANS still has SUPLPHOS=NONE, the transient run will immediately re-impose P starvation and undo the spinup progress. Both phases should be set to ALL to allow proper equilibration. Post-spinup diagnostic P mass balance should be re-run to confirm supply/demand ratio is reasonable (target: >0.1) before any parameter optimization.
- **Requires re-spinup:** Yes

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- vmax_nh4_7 vs PFT9/PFT10 competition: Reducing PFT7's NH4 uptake capacity (item 1) will benefit PFT10 more than PFT9, because PFT9 already has higher relative competitive advantage. Monitor that PFT9_fineroot does not increase excessively (currently passing at 223.8 vs target 187.35 — risk of overcorrection).
- km_nh4 reduction for both PFT9 and PFT10: If both PFT9 km_nh4 and PFT10 km_nh4 are reduced simultaneously, they become more competitive at low N concentrations but compete more intensely with each other. Recommend applying PFT10 km reduction first (priority 2) and PFT9 km reduction only after confirming PFT10 improvement (priority 6).
- l2fr_ini_9 reduction and PFT9/PFT10 light competition: Reducing l2fr_ini_9 increases PFT9 leaf area, which may shade PFT10. In Arctic tundra with short-statured PFT10, this is a real concern. However, PFT7 (taller shrubs) are likely the dominant shaders — PFT9 shading PFT10 is secondary concern.
- recruit_init_density_10 increase vs soil nutrient depletion: More PFT10 individuals increase total PFT10 P demand. At the extreme upper bound, this could worsen P starvation for all PFTs. The recommended moderate increase (0.100→0.281, within ensemble range) should not catastrophically increase demand, but needs monitoring.
