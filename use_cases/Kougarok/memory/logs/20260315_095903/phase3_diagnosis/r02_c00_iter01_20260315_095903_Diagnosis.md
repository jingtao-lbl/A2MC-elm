## Likely Causes

1. SYSTEMIC P STARVATION (PRIMARY): Total plant P demand (358,121 g/m²/yr) exceeds total P uptake (0.67 g/m²/yr) by a factor of ~530,000x. Supply/demand ratios are 0.000 for PFT7, 0.000 for PFT9, and 0.001 for PFT10. This is not a calibration failure — it is a near-complete P cutoff that prevents all biomass accumulation regardless of parameter settings.
2. PROTOCOL-LEVEL P STARVATION (PRIMARY-STRUCTURAL): Current spinup configuration has A2MC_RGSP_SUPLPHOS=NONE and A2MC_TRANS_SUPLPHOS=NONE, while A2MC_ADSP_SUPLPHOS=ALL. Vegetation builds biomass with supplemented P in ADSP but then collapses when P supplementation is removed in RGSP/TRANS phases, because soil P mineralization rates (gross: 0.034 g/m²/yr, biochem: 0.533 g/m²/yr) are insufficient to sustain the observed biomass levels under current stoichiometry parameters.
3. PARAMETER SPACE BOUNDARY CONSTRAINT: Case #322 (best target satisfier) has 46 of 162 parameters (28.4%) at or near sampling bounds, with 6 CNP vmax parameters and 4 CNP km parameters specifically at bounds. The test_morris_bounds_impact confirmed this at 95% confidence with 9/9 key parameters at bounds. This proves the current ensemble sampling does not bracket the feasible parameter region.
4. P DEMAND OVER-PARAMETERIZATION: Plant P demand is computed from fineroot biomass × vmax_p. With PFT7 fineroot at 63 g/m² and vmax_p_7 at 2.86e-5 kg/kg/s, annual P demand ≈ 192,556 g/m²/yr — ~200,000x the actual soil P supply. Either stoichiometric targets (fates_stoich_phos) are too high, or vmax_p values are set such that even at low biomass the demand calculation returns unrealistically large values.
5. L2FR-DRIVEN CARBON ALLOCATION CONFLICT (SECONDARY): The L2FR carbon limitation test (confidence 0.60) shows high L2FR (>10) reduces PFT9 leaves by 63%, with 48% of PFT9 cases and 43% of PFT10 cases showing carbon limitation patterns. Case #322 has l2fr_ini_9=18.31 (at upper bound) and l2fr_ini_10=9.88 (at upper bound), creating excessive root carbon demand that depletes leaf C pools.
6. P EFFLUX EXCEEDS MINERALIZATION INPUT: Plant P efflux (0.4285 g/m²/yr) is 38.4% of total P output and exceeds the combined weathering + deposition input (0.0008 g/m²/yr) by 536x. The system is losing more P via efflux than it gains from primary inputs, sustained only by desorption from secondary P pools that are already declining (SECONDP: 124.99 → 92.53, -26% over 119 years).

## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_stoich_phos (PFT7: stoich_phos_leaf_7, stoich_phos_fineroot_7; PFT9: stoich_phos_leaf_9, stoich_phos_fineroot_9; PFT10: stoich_phos_leaf_10, stoich_phos_fineroot_10) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** decrease lower and upper bounds by 50% for initial test; explore range [0.0005, 0.0015] for leaf P and [0.0004, 0.0012] for fineroot P
- **Issue:** Plant P demand is 358,121 g/m²/yr vs supply of 0.67 g/m²/yr. The stoichiometric P targets set the demand ceiling — even at low biomass the demand calculation returns catastrophically high values relative to soil supply. Current ranges (0.001-0.003 gP/gC) may need reduction to bring demand into alignment with Arctic soil P availability.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (vmax_p_7, vmax_p_9, vmax_p_10) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** decrease all vmax_p values; expand lower bound downward by 2 orders of magnitude to 5e-12 for PFT10, 5e-11 for PFT9; consider 1e-10 lower bound for PFT7
- **Issue:** P demand = fineroot_C × vmax_p. With Case #322 having vmax_p_10 at lower bound (5e-11) yet PFT10 is still catastrophically P-limited (demand 15.5 g/m²/yr vs uptake 0.013 g/m²/yr), the vmax_p values for PFT7 and PFT9 are far too high. Case #322 vmax_p_7=2.86e-5 creates demand of 192,556 g/m²/yr for PFT7 alone.

### fates_allom_l2fr (l2fr_ini_9, l2fr_ini_10) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** For PFT9: reduce upper bound from 18.31 to 8.0; expand lower bound to 0.5. For PFT10: maintain high l2fr (observationally justified for graminoids) but verify that l2fr_ini drives allocation correctly in FATES PARTEH — the initial ratio may not be the equilibrium ratio
- **Issue:** Case #322 has l2fr_ini_9=18.31 (at upper bound 18.31) and l2fr_ini_10=9.88 (at upper bound 9.88). The L2FR test (confidence 0.60) shows high L2FR reduces PFT9 leaves by 63%. Arctic graminoids (PFT10) have high root:shoot ratios observationally, but l2fr_ini=9.88 may be creating excessive initial carbon investment in roots at the expense of leaf area development.

### fates_cnp_turnover_phos_retrans (phos_retrans_7, phos_retrans_9, phos_retrans_10) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** For phos_retrans_10: decrease lower bound from 0.7 to 0.5 to allow exploring lower retranslocation scenarios; for phos_retrans_7: test whether lowering from 0.9 reduces PFT7's competitive advantage and allows PFT9/10 more P access
- **Issue:** Case #322 has phos_retrans_7=0.9 (at upper bound), phos_retrans_9=0.714, phos_retrans_10=0.7 (at lower bound). High retranslocation in PFT7 may help it dominate P cycling (73.4% of total uptake share) at expense of PFT9 and PFT10. Arctic plants typically have high P retranslocation (0.7-0.9 range is appropriate) but the asymmetry between PFTs creates competitive imbalance.

### fates_cnp_eca_vmax_ptase (vmax_ptase_9, vmax_ptase_10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** Maintain or slightly increase upper bound for vmax_ptase_9 and vmax_ptase_10; do NOT prioritize phosphatase tuning until P demand-supply balance is addressed first — phosphatase increases will be futile if stoichiometric demand is 530,000x supply
- **Issue:** Case #322 has vmax_ptase_9=5e-10 (at lower bound) and vmax_ptase_10=5e-10 (at lower bound). Biochemical P mineralization (0.533 g/m²/yr) is the largest single P input to the system. Increasing phosphatase production for PFT9 and PFT10 could increase organic P access, but the test_p_cascade showed negative correlation (confidence -0.07), suggesting phosphatase is not the primary bottleneck in current parameterization.

## Protocol Recommendations

### A2MC_RGSP_SUPLPHOS (Priority: 1)
- **Current:** `NONE` → **Proposed:** `ALL`
- **Rationale:** The RGSP (regular spinup) phase currently has zero P supplementation, but soil P mineralization rates are insufficient to sustain the vegetation biomass levels observed at Kougarok. SMINP is declining from 125.4 to 92.9 g/m² over 119 years, indicating the system cannot reach a P-sufficient equilibrium under current parameters. Supplementing P during RGSP allows vegetation to equilibrate to realistic biomass levels, revealing whether the model structure CAN reproduce observed biomass when not P-starved. This is a DIAGNOSTIC protocol change to separate structural model adequacy from nutrient cycling calibration.
- **Requires re-spinup:** Yes

### A2MC_TRANS_SUPLPHOS (Priority: 2)
- **Current:** `NONE` → **Proposed:** `ALL`
- **Rationale:** Same rationale as RGSP. The transition spinup should also supplement P to allow vegetation to reach biomass equilibrium before the observational period. Once correct biomass is achieved, subsequent experiments can test whether P cycling parameters (vmax_p, stoich_phos, phosphatase) maintain that biomass without supplementation. If vegetation collapses immediately upon P supplementation removal, this confirms stoichiometric parameters need fundamental revision.
- **Requires re-spinup:** Yes

### A2MC_ADSP_NYEARS_AD_CARBON_ONLY (Priority: 3)
- **Current:** `30` → **Proposed:** `50`
- **Rationale:** If P supplementation changes are adopted, extending the carbon-only spinup phase by 20 years ensures vegetation reaches carbon equilibrium before nutrient cycling begins, preventing initial C deficit from being confounded with P limitation signals. This is a lower-priority adjustment, contingent on the SUPLPHOS changes.
- **Requires re-spinup:** Yes

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- PFT7 dominates P competition (73.4% of total P uptake = 0.492 g/m²/yr) at the expense of PFT9 (24.6% = 0.165 g/m²/yr) and PFT10 (2.0% = 0.013 g/m²/yr). This inter-PFT competitive asymmetry means that parameter changes improving PFT7 (e.g., increasing vmax_nh4_7 which is at upper bound in Case #322) will further disadvantage PFT9 and PFT10 in ECA competition.
- l2fr_ini_9=18.31 (at upper bound) and l2fr_ini_10=9.88 (at upper bound) create massive fine root carbon sinks that compete with PFT7 for both carbon and nutrients. However, reducing L2FR for PFT10 conflicts with the ecological reality that Arctic graminoids have high root:shoot ratios (R:S often 5-15 for Eriophorum, Carex species).
- phen_mindaysoff_7=148.6 and phen_mindaysoff_10=148.6 (both at upper bound in Case #322) extend dormancy periods, reducing annual carbon gain for these PFTs. This lengthened dormancy combined with P starvation creates a compounding effect: less GPP × longer dormancy = less annual carbon available for biomass investment.
- The vmax_nh4_7=0.00025 (at upper bound) combined with microb_bio_7=600 (at upper bound) gives PFT7 maximum competitive advantage in ECA NH4 uptake, potentially suppressing N availability for PFT9 and PFT10 through microbial competition effects. While N is currently supplemented (A2MC_ADSP_SUPLNITRO=NONE), this inter-PFT N competition may become relevant if P starvation is resolved.
- stoich_nitr_leaf_9=0.0582 (at upper bound) is the highest N stoichiometry in the ensemble. High N stoichiometry increases N demand proportionally to biomass, creating a potential N-P co-limitation scenario for PFT9 that is currently masked by the overwhelming P limitation signal.
