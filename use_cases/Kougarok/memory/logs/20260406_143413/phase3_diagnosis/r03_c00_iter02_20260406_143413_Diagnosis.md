## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_stoich_phos (leaf, all PFTs — especially PFT10 and PFT9) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** decrease (extend lower bounds below current minimum for PFT10 leaf and fineroot; reduce from upper bounds for PFT9 leaf and fineroot)
- **Issue:** P demand computed from stoich_phos × leaf_C is 126,187 gP/m²/yr total vs uptake of 5.59 gP/m²/yr — a 22,000× gap that is structurally unachievable regardless of vmax_p settings. In Case #86: stoich_phos_leaf_9=0.00428 (upper bound), stoich_phos_fineroot_9=0.00207 (upper bound), stoich_phos_leaf_10=0.000921 (lower bound — already at minimum, suggesting current lower bound is still too high for Arctic conditions). Arctic tundra literature for graminoids (Carex, Eriophorum) shows leaf P:C of 0.0010-0.0020 gP/gC and root P:C of 0.0005-0.0009 gP/gC — below current Morris lower bounds.

### fates_cnp_eca_decompmicc (PFT7: microb_bio_7, PFT9: microb_bio_9, PFT10: microb_bio_10) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** decrease (toward lower half of range [140-280 gC/m³]) for PFT7 and PFT9
- **Issue:** In Case #86: microb_bio_7=468.6 gC/m³ (upper half of [140, 600] range), microb_bio_9=468.6 gC/m³. Morris rank #2 for PFT7 fineroot (μ*=0.124). High microbial biomass concentrations in ECA framework reduce plant P access by outcompeting roots. The ECA solver allocates soil P proportionally to km-weighted biomass of all competitors — high decompmicc systematically suppresses plant uptake fraction. Arctic tundra microbial biomass concentrations in organic soils are variable but often 100-300 gC/m³ in upper active layer. Current upper range (600 gC/m³) may be too high.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT10: vmax_p_10) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** increase (extend upper bound to 1.5e-04 to 2e-04 for PFT10)
- **Issue:** In Case #86: vmax_p_10=3.57e-05 gP/gC/s (within [5e-11, 5e-05] range but effective P uptake/demand ≈ 0.00). In Case #4670 where PFT10 collapses completely: vmax_p_10=5e-11 (lower bound). Morris sensitivity for PFT9 abg shows vmax_p_10 at rank 9 (μ*=0.095 cross-PFT signal). The upper bound (5e-05) may be insufficient — Arctic graminoids with dense, fine-root mats and mycorrhizal associations can achieve high specific P uptake rates. Consider extending upper bound for PFT10 to 2e-04.

### fates_cnp_phos_store_ratio (PFT10: phos_store_ratio_10) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** decrease to 1.0-1.5 (minimum storage buffer) for PFT10
- **Issue:** In Case #86: phos_store_ratio_10=5.0 (upper bound). Morris rank #3 for PFT10 fineroot (μ*=0.041). At 5.0×, the model targets 5× structural P in labile storage — when P is scarce, the PID controller prioritizes storage filling over growth allocation, diverting the small amount of P that IS taken up into STOREP rather than structural tissues. In Case #4670: phos_store_ratio_10=2.14 but PFT10 still collapses because vmax_p_10 is at lower bound. The interaction between phos_store_ratio and vmax_p must be co-optimized.

### fates_cnp_turnover_phos_retrans (PFT7: phos_retrans_7, PFT10: phos_retrans_10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** increase phos_retrans_7 (lower bound should be extended from 0.6 to allow optimization toward 0.75-0.85)
- **Issue:** In Case #86: phos_retrans_7=0.60 (at LOWER bound of [0.60, 0.90] range). This is the minimum allowed P retranslocation for PFT7. With P starvation confirmed for PFT7, maximizing retranslocation from senescing leaves reduces the net P demand from soil. phos_retrans_10=0.871 (near upper bound), already near-optimal. The critical underperformance is for PFT7 where retranslocation is at minimum — Arctic evergreen shrubs in nutrient-poor environments typically show high retranslocation (0.70-0.85).

### fates_phen_gddthresh_c (shared across all PFTs) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** explore values toward more negative end of range (-0.012 to -0.015) to shorten effective growing season and reduce cumulative P demand
- **Issue:** Morris rank #1 for ALL output variables across PFT7, PFT9, PFT10 (abg: μ*=0.791/0.440/0.144; leaf: μ*=0.124/0.031/0.020; froot: μ*=0.158/0.142/0.059). In Case #86: phen_gddthresh_c=-0.01074 (within [-0.01492, -0.00517] range). The dominant sensitivity of this shared parameter means ANY calibration attempt must carefully optimize it. A more negative value (toward -0.01492) delays leaf-out, reducing annual P demand duration and potentially allowing P stores to partially recover. However, changing this affects all PFTs simultaneously.

### fates_cnp_eca_km_nh4 (PFT7: km_nh4_7) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** decrease (allow exploration below 0.21 — consider extending upper bound or finding optimal within current range)
- **Issue:** In Case #86: km_nh4_7=0.21 gN/m³ at UPPER bound. High Km means lower affinity for NH4, reducing N uptake at given NH4 concentrations. While N is not the primary limiting nutrient (P is confirmed as primary), N co-limitation through ECA dynamics may compound the P starvation — inadequate N uptake reduces fine-root biomass, which in turn reduces P uptake capacity. The N:P ratio of uptake vs demand may be mismatched.

This is the most fundamental cross-PFT conflict in the system.
- CONFIRMED: phen_gddthresh_c (shared parameter, rank #1 for ALL 6 biomass targets across all PFTs) creates inescapable cross-PFT dependency — any adjustment to optimize one PFT's phenological timing affects all others simultaneously. In Case #86: phen_gddthresh_c=-0.01074; in Case #4670 (same 3 targets but PFT10 collapse): phen_gddthresh_c=-0.01492 (lower bound) — more negative value shifts PFT balance toward PFT7/PFT9.
- INTERACTION: In Case #86, high stoich_phos_fineroot_9=0.00207 (upper bound) creates higher PFT9 P demand, which through shared ECA soil P pool depletes P available to PFT10 and PFT7. Reducing PFT9 stoich_phos reduces PFT9's competitive P demand, potentially freeing P for PFT10 without directly tuning PFT10 parameters.
- INTERACTION: microb_bio_7=468.6 and microb_bio_9=468.6 in Case #86 — both in upper half of range — collectively suppress plant P uptake across ALL PFTs through the shared ECA solver. Reducing microbial biomass parameters would broadly improve P access but cannot be targeted to one PFT since all share the same soil P competition framework.

For Case #86 to satisfy PFT7_leaf (simulated=31.0 vs obs=24.55 — 26% overshoot), turnover_leaf_7 should DECREASE. But PFT7_fineroot already satisfies target (150.6 vs 174.25). This creates an intra-PFT7 tension: reducing leaf turnover would bring PFT7_leaf closer to target but affects whole-plant carbon balance.
