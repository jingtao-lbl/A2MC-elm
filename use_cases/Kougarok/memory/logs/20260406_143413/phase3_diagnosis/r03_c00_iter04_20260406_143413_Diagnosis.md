## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- CRITICAL (Cycle 3 CONFIRMED): PFT9-PFT10 P competition trade-off — reducing stoich_phos_leaf_9 frees soil P for PFT10 (r=-0.122 PFT10 leaf correlation, 2.95x improvement in dual-low group) BUT PFT9 leaf collapses in dual-low group (pft9_preserved=False, Cycle 3). This is the primary unsolved conflict requiring the novel nfix1_9 compensation mechanism.
- PFT7-PFT9-PFT10 shared soil P pool: All three PFTs compete for the same depleted LABILEP/SMINP pools. Any reduction in one PFT's microbial competition (microb_bio_7, microb_bio_9) or stoichiometric demand (stoich_phos_leaf_9) benefits all three PFTs via the shared pool, but the magnitude differs. PFT7 appears most competitive (63.2% of total P uptake in Case #86 despite P starvation) potentially out-competing PFT9 and PFT10.
- turnover_leaf_7 (PFT7 leaf fix) ↔ PFT7_fineroot (currently satisfied): Reducing turnover_leaf_7 from 2.0 to 1.5 yr reduces litter input and may reduce organic P cycling via decomposition, potentially degrading the already-marginal PFT7_fineroot (150.7 vs 174.3, within ±20% but below obs). Monitor carefully.
- nfix1_9 (proposed increase) ↔ N stoichiometry: Increasing nitrogen fixation for PFT9 raises total ecosystem N availability, which could shift N:P ratios and affect the N limitation status of PFT7 and PFT10. With P remaining limited, adding more N without commensurate P may exacerbate nutrient imbalance or trigger luxury N consumption in ways that don't help biomass.
- phen_gddthresh_c shared parameter: Morris rank #1 for ALL three PFTs (PFT7 abg μ*=0.791, PFT9 abg μ*=0.440, PFT10 abg μ*=0.144). Currently at -0.01074 in Case #86. Any phenology adjustment propagates across all PFTs simultaneously and is not a PFT-specific fix — should not be modified until single-PFT effects are characterized.
