## AI Reasoning and Deep Analysis

The diagnosis integrates four converging evidence streams: (1) verified diagnostic tool outputs showing universal P-limitation across all three PFTs (uptake/demand ≈ 0.00 for PFT7, PFT9, PFT10), (2) a catastrophically failed P mass balance with -507% relative residual indicating systemic P depletion of soil pools, (3) Case #86 edge parameter analysis confirming 39 parameters pressing against bounds with P-uptake parameters dominant, and (4) the specific biomass collapse pattern in PFT10 (leaf: 4.7 vs 82.7 gC/m², fineroot: 22.6 vs 382.1 gC/m²). The causal chain is: soil P pools depleted during spinup → ECA competition produces near-zero plant uptake vs demand → P starvation blocks PARTEH allocation → leaf and fineroot biomass collapse → targets unachievable via parameter tuning alone.

Conceptual model:
  Soil P Pools (SMINP, LABILEP) --> [ECA competition: decompmicc vs plant vmax] --> Plant P Uptake

       |                                                            PFT7 moderate (froot -14% in #86)

The P budget closure failure (residual -507% of total input) is the smoking gun: plant P efflux (5.17 gC/m²/yr) nearly equals plant P uptake (5.58 gC/m²/yr), meaning almost all P taken up is immediately re-effluxed — a sign of thermodynamic P cycling with near-zero net assimilation. Soil mineral P declined from 514 to 320 gC/m² (-194 units) despite supplementation during ADSP/RGSP phases, confirming irreversible pool drawdown during transient.

The RGSP/ADSP supplementation (SUPLPHOS=ALL) failed to build adequate P capital: by the time transient begins, vegetation demand (126,187 gC/m²/yr total — an extraordinarily high value suggesting units or scaling issue worth investigating) vastly exceeds any realistic supply. The 100x discrepancy between demand and uptake is not correctable through vmax or km tuning alone within current ranges — the system needs either (a) extended spinup with supplementation to build plant P stores, or (b) reduced stoichiometric P demand (stoich_phos parameters), or (c) both.

## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_stoich_phos (leaf and fineroot, all PFTs) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** P demand computed from stoich_phos × leaf_C is 126,187 gC/m²/yr total vs uptake of 5.59 gC/m²/yr — a 22,000x gap. Even if the units interpretation causes some scaling, the ratio indicates stoichiometric targets set far above what Arctic soil P supply can support. stoich_phos_leaf_9 and stoich_phos_fineroot_9 at upper bounds in Case #86 exacerbates this.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (all PFTs, especially PFT10) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** vmax_p_10 at lower bound in Case #86 (5e-11, essentially zero) — PFT10 graminoid has minimal P uptake capacity. Morris sensitivity shows phos_store_ratio_10 (μ*=0.041) and stoich_nitr_fineroot_10 (μ*=0.040) as top fineroot drivers for PFT10, indicating P storage and N stoichiometry matter, but P uptake rate itself must first be adequate.

### fates_cnp_eca_decompmicc (PFT7: microb_bio_7, PFT9: microb_bio_9) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** In Case #86: microb_bio_7=468 (upper half of range), microb_bio_9=468. High microbial biomass competes with plants for P in ECA framework. Morris analysis shows microb_bio_7 is rank #2 for PFT7 fineroot (μ*=0.124). Microbial competition may be systematically preventing plant P access.

### fates_cnp_phos_store_ratio (PFT10: phos_store_ratio_10) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** phos_store_ratio_10=5.0 at upper bound in Case #86. Morris analysis shows phos_store_ratio_10 (μ*=0.041) affects PFT10 fineroot. Maximum P storage target set to 5× structural P creates a large P sink that diverts uptake away from growth when P is already severely limiting.

### fates_allom_l2fr (l2fr_ini_9, PFT9) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** l2fr_ini_9=13.08 in Case #86 is very high (upper range 18.3). Diagnostic test_l2fr_carbon_limitation shows: high L2FR (>10) reduces PFT9 leaves by 63%. Carbon allocated excessively to fine roots with 48% of PFT9 cases showing carbon limitation pattern. This interacts with P limitation: more roots should improve P uptake, but if soil P is depleted, roots cannot help.

### fates_turnover_leaf (turnover_leaf_7) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** increase upper bound
- **Issue:** turnover_leaf_7=2.0 at upper bound in Case #86. Morris analysis: turnover_leaf_7 is rank #2 for PFT7 abg biomass (μ*=0.436) and leaf biomass (μ*=0.060). Maximum leaf longevity for evergreen shrub may need to extend beyond 2 years — Arctic evergreen shrubs like Ledum/Rhododendron can have leaf lifespans of 3-5 years.

### fates_phen_gddthresh_c (shared) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** optimize carefully with multi-PFT awareness
- **Issue:** Dominant sensitivity parameter across all PFTs and outputs (μ*=0.79 for PFT7 abg, 0.44 for PFT9 abg, 0.14 for PFT10 abg). In Case #86: phen_gddthresh_c=-0.01074. This shared parameter controls growing season length for all PFTs simultaneously — any change propagates across all three PFTs.

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- phen_gddthresh_c is shared across PFT7, PFT9, PFT10 and is the most sensitive parameter in the ensemble. Any change affects growing season length for all three PFTs simultaneously, making it impossible to independently tune phenological timing per PFT.
- ECA competition creates direct inter-PFT P competition: PFT7 dominates P uptake (63.2% share), PFT9 gets only 5.5% despite needing 39,944 gC/m²/yr. Increasing vmax_p for PFT9 or PFT10 will compete with PFT7, and vice versa.
- nitr_retrans_7 appears as sensitive for PFT10 abg biomass (μ*=0.035) — a cross-PFT artifact where PFT7 N cycling influences PFT10 through shared soil N pools, confirming inter-PFT N competition is active.
- microb_bio_7 affects PFT7 fineroot (rank #2, μ*=0.124) but microbial biomass is a shared soil pool competing with all PFTs — reducing microb_bio_7 benefits all plants, not just PFT7.
- stoich_nitr_leaf_9 is rank #3 for PFT7 fineroot biomass (μ*=0.126) — PFT9 N demand directly affects PFT7 root allocation, confirming tight inter-PFT N coupling.
