- Winner-Loser P Competition (CONFIRMED, Cycles 3-4): Reducing stoich_phos_leaf_9 (Priority 1) frees soil P for PFT10 via ECA redistribution, but simultaneously causes PFT9 leaf collapse in Cycle 3 (pft9_preserved_in_dual_low=False). The winner-loser dynamic cannot be fully resolved within the ensemble without spinup protocol fix.
- PFT7 Dominance in P Uptake (CONFIRMED): PFT7 captures 63.2% of total P uptake (3.53 vs PFT9 0.31 + PFT10 1.75 gP/m²/yr). Any reduction in microbial competition or increase in PFT7 P access risks further widening the PFT7-PFT10 uptake gap. Reducing microb_bio_7 conservatively (to 270, not 150) prevents PFT7 from becoming even more dominant. Simultaneously, phos_retrans_7 increase (Priority 9) reduces PFT7 net soil P demand, partially counterbalancing the microb_bio_7 competition relief.
- Case #1385 PFT7 Collapse Trade-off: Case #1385 achieves PFT10 leaf=69.5 gC/m² but PFT7_fineroot collapses to 15.6 gC/m² (vs 174.3 target). This confirms that extreme cross-PFT P demand reduction (stoich_phos_leaf_9 + microb_bio_9 to minimum) rescues PFT10 at the cost of PFT7. The proposed balanced intervention uses moderate microb_bio_7=270 (not minimum) and retranslocation increase to compensate, aiming to prevent PFT7_fineroot degradation while enabling PFT10 recovery.
- phen_gddthresh_c Shared Phenology Conflict (CONFIRMED, Phase 2): phen_gddthresh_c is the #1 most sensitive parameter for all three PFTs across all biomass types (PFT7 abg μ*=0.791, PFT9 abg μ*=0.440, PFT10 abg μ*=0.144). This is NOT proposed for modification in Cycle 5 experiment to avoid introducing confounding phenology effects while testing the P mechanism.
- nitr_store_ratio_10 vs Case #4670 Pattern: Case #4670 has nitr_store_ratio_10=1.57 (low storage) and satisfies PFT7_leaf + PFT9_leaf + PFT9_fineroot but shows PFT10 completely collapsed (leaf=0.17, froot=0.17 gC/m²). This counter-intuitive result suggests that reduced N storage in PFT10 alone does NOT rescue it — the P deficit dominates. Confirmed secondary role of storage parameters.
