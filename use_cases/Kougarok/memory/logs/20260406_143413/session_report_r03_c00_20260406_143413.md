## Executive Summary

Calibration Round 3 deployed 162 parameters across 4,890 Morris ensemble simulations at the Kougarok Arctic tundra site, targeting leaf and fineroot biomass for three co-occurring plant functional types (PFT7: evergreen shrub, PFT9: deciduous shrub, PFT10: graminoid). Screening revealed a severe calibration challenge: no ensemble member achieved more than 3 of 6 targets, and 80.9% of cases met zero targets, pointing to a structural model failure rather than simple parameter mis-specification. Seven skip-testing iterations of diagnosis and hypothesis refinement progressively identified **catastrophic phosphorus starvation** as the universal root cause — driven by a spinup-protocol failure in which the 30-year accelerated decomposition (ADSP) carbon-only phase grows substantial biomass before P cycling is activated, creating a latent P debt that the RGSP supplementation phase cannot repay. The first HPC experiment cycle (12 cumulative experiments addressing cross-PFT P competition and N stoichiometry co-limitation) failed to improve any target, with all 12 experiments meeting 0/6 targets — confirming that parameter tuning alone, operating within the current spinup protocol, cannot overcome the structural P supply deficit. The session concludes that a spinup protocol redesign (reducing the carbon-only phase and/or extending RGSP supplementation duration) is the necessary structural fix before parameter calibration can proceed meaningfully.

### Iteration 2

**Diagnosis Confidence:** 0.88  
**New Finding: PFT10 Recovery Is Achievable**

The second iteration incorporated a critical new observation: Case #1385 (rank 9 by RMSRE) achieves **PFT10 leaf = 69.5 gC/m²** (84% of target), while Cases #86 and #4670 show PFT10 leaf = 4.7 and 0.17 gC/m² respectively. This proved that PFT10 recovery is mechanistically achievable within the current ensemble — the challenge is not a fundamental structural model failure but a multi-objective conflict in which improving one PFT's P access degrades others.

**Confirmed cross-PFT P competition:** Cases achieving PFT10 biomass (#1385) completely fail PFT7 (leaf = 11.6 vs. 24.6 gC/m²; froot = 15.6 vs. 174.2 gC/m²) and PFT9 (leaf = 31.2 vs. 124.7 gC/m²). The shared soil P pool creates a zero-sum competition where improving one PFT's access necessarily degrades others.

Additional confirmed causes in Iteration 2:
- `phos_store_ratio_10 = 5.0` (upper bound in Case #86) was identified as creating a "P storage trap" — the scarce P that IS taken up is directed into labile storage (STOREP) rather than structural tissue, preventing biomass construction
- `stoich_phos_leaf_9 = 0.00428` (upper Morris bound in Case #86) creates disproportionate PFT9 leaf P demand that monopolizes the shared soil P pool

### Iteration 3

**Diagnosis Confidence:** 0.82  
**New Finding: Case #1385 Fingerprint — Cross-PFT P Release Mechanism**

The third iteration performed a parameter-value fingerprint comparison between Case #86 (PFT10 leaf = 4.7 gC/m²) and Case #1385 (PFT10 leaf = 69.5 gC/m²). The most diagnostically significant differentiators were:

| Parameter | Case #86 | Case #1385 | Relative Change |
|-----------|----------|------------|----------------|
| `stoich_phos_leaf_9` | 0.00428 (upper bound) | 0.00210 (lower bound) | −51% |
| `microb_bio_9` | 468.6 gC/m³ | 140.0 gC/m³ (lower bound) | −70% |
| `phos_retrans_7` | 0.60 (lower bound) | 0.857 | +43% |

**Key mechanistic insight:** PFT10 recovery in Case #1385 is achieved NOT by changing PFT10-specific parameters but by reducing **PFT9's P competitive advantage** through lower stoichiometric demand and lower microbial competition. This is a cross-PFT mechanism mediated by the shared ECA soil P pool.

Skip-testing in Iteration 3 confirmed:
- **Correlation r = −0.122** between `stoich_phos_leaf_9` and PFT10 leaf biomass across the ensemble
- **Correlation r = −0.158** between `microb_bio_9` and PFT10 leaf biomass (strongest single-parameter correlation found)
- Dual-low group (stoich_phos_leaf_9 at lower tertile + microb_bio_9 at lower tertile) achieves **2.95× more PFT10 leaf** vs. dual-high group

However, skip-testing also revealed the critical trade-off: PFT9 leaf **collapses** in the dual-low group — confirming a fundamental winner-loser dynamic in ECA P competition.

### Iteration 4

**Diagnosis Confidence:** 0.83  
**New Hypothesis: nfix1_9 as PFT9 Compensation Lever**

The fourth iteration introduced a novel mechanistic hypothesis: if reducing `stoich_phos_leaf_9` frees soil P for PFT10 but collapses PFT9 leaf, then increasing `nfix1_9` (nitrogen fixation rate for PFT9) could provide a **P-independent carbon allocation pathway** for PFT9 recovery. This would decouple PFT9 leaf biomass from soil P competition status.

Supporting evidence:
- `nfix1_9` is **Morris rank #2 for PFT9 abg biomass** (μ* = 0.282) and rank #2 for PFT9 leaf (μ* = 0.015)
- Skip-testing confirmed: **r = +0.211** between `nfix1_9` and PFT9 abg biomass

The "triple-lever" group (low `stoich_phos_leaf_9` + low `microb_bio_9` + high `nfix1_9`) showed **PFT9 leaf +189.6%** vs. the dual-low-lownfix group, confirming that N-fixation can compensate for reduced P-driven leaf construction.

Additional finding: **turnover_leaf_7** at upper bound (2.0 yr) in Case #86 is causing a **+26% PFT7 leaf overshoot** (31.0 vs. 24.55 gC/m² target). Correlation r = +0.068 confirmed, and the low-turnover7 group showed 43% lower PFT7 leaf biomass — directly actionable.

**Not confirmed in Iteration 4:** The `phos_store_ratio_10` storage trap hypothesis showed a counterintuitive weak positive correlation (r = +0.058) between `phos_store_ratio_10` and PFT10 froot biomass. This was diagnosed as a confounding effect: under universal P starvation where uptake/demand ≈ 0, the storage ratio has no effective target — the storage trap mechanism becomes relevant only after primary P supply is partially restored.

## Key Discoveries

### Discovery 1: Spinup-Protocol-Induced P Starvation Cascade

**Named:** "ADSP Carbon-Only P Debt"

The 30-year accelerated decomposition spinup (ADSP) carbon-only phase allows vegetation biomass to grow unconstrained by nutrient availability. When P cycling activates at the end of this phase, the established biomass exerts immediate P demand that exceeds soil P supply. The RGSP P supplementation phase (`RGSP_SUPLPHOS = ALL`) is insufficient to replenish the P consumed by this biomass pulse. By the time the transient simulation begins with `TRANS_SUPLPHOS = NONE` (scientifically required), the soil P pools are catastrophically depleted: LABILEP = 0.00 gP/m² (100% depleted) and SMINP = 320 gP/m² (37.7% below initial).

This creates a P starvation cascade in the transient: zero labile P → ECA solver produces plant P uptake/demand ≈ 0 for all PFTs → PARTEH P-starvation mode → all structural carbon allocation suspended → leaf and fineroot biomass collapse for all three PFTs.

**Implication:** No parameter calibration within the transient phase can resolve this structural deficit. The fix must occur at the spinup protocol level.

---

### Discovery 2: ECA Competition Asymmetry — PFT7 Dominates P Access

**Named:** "PFT7 ECA Dominance"

Under the current parameter configuration, PFT7 (Arctic evergreen shrub) captures **63.2% of total plant P uptake** despite all three PFTs being P-starved. This creates a structural asymmetry in the ECA (Equilibrium Chemistry Approximation) solver where PFT7's higher vmax × root biomass product outcompetes PFT9 and PFT10 for the scarce soil P. PFT9 receives only ~5.5% of plant P uptake despite having comparable or higher P demand (through `stoich_phos_leaf_9` at upper bound). PFT10 receives essentially nothing.

This asymmetry means that improving PFT10 P access requires either (a) reducing PFT9's competitive P demand (the Case #1385 mechanism), (b) reducing microbial competition (`microb_bio_9` reduction), or (c) a combination of both — and that either intervention creates a cross-PFT trade-off where PFT9 simultaneously loses its marginal P access.

---

### Discovery 3: Cross-PFT P Release Mechanism Confirmed but Insufficient

**Named:** "Winner-Loser ECA P Trade-Off"

Case #1385 fingerprint analysis revealed that PFT10 recovery (leaf: 69.5 gC/m², 84% of target) is achieved through **cross-PFT P demand reduction** — specifically reducing PFT9 leaf P stoichiometry (`stoich_phos_leaf_9`: 0.00428 → 0.00210) and reducing PFT9 microbial competition (`microb_bio_9`: 468.6 → 140 gC/m³). This frees shared ECA soil P for PFT10 uptake.

Correlations confirmed: r = −0.122 (stoich_phos_leaf_9 vs. PFT10 leaf) and r = −0.158 (microb_bio_9 vs. PFT10 leaf, the strongest single-parameter correlation in the entire diagnosis). The dual-low group achieved 2.95× more PFT10 leaf than the dual-high group.

However, this mechanism operates on the distribution of a severely depleted P pool. Even multiplying PFT10's share by 3× when the total is ~5% of demand still leaves recovery far short of the target. The mechanism is real and necessary but insufficient without P supply restoration.

### Discovery 4: N-Fixation as P-Independent PFT9 Compensation

**Named:** "nfix1_9 Decoupling Mechanism"

When `stoich_phos_leaf_9` is reduced to free P for PFT10, PFT9 leaf biomass collapses in the Morris ensemble. Increasing `nfix1_9` (0.571 → 0.90) provides a P-independent carbon allocation pathway for PFT9, allowing leaf construction via N-sufficient photosynthesis even when soil P competition status is reduced.

Confirmed: r = +0.211 between `nfix1_9` and PFT9 abg biomass; triple-lever group shows PFT9 leaf +189.6% vs. dual-low-lownfix group. This is a mechanistically novel finding — N-fixation rate as a lever for maintaining biomass when stoichiometric demand is reduced.

Caveat: The biological interpretation of `nfix1_9 = 0.90` for a graminoid PFT requires validation. If PFT9 represents a graminoid or non-N-fixing shrub rather than an alder or legume type, this parameter may not be ecologically defensible.

### Discovery 5: P Storage Trap — Conditional Effect

**Named:** "PARTEH P Storage Trap (Protocol-Dependent)"

`phos_store_ratio_10 = 5.0` (upper Morris bound in Case #86) creates a PARTEH allocation rule requiring 5× structural P in labile storage before structural growth can proceed. Under P starvation (uptake/demand ≈ 0), this trap is irrelevant — there is no P to divert. The storage trap becomes operationally important only after primary P supply is partially restored by protocol or competition-level interventions.

This was confirmed by the counterintuitive finding that `phos_store_ratio_10` showed a weak **positive** correlation with PFT10 biomass (r = +0.058) in the ensemble — indicating that cases where `phos_store_ratio_10` is high tend to have other parameters (not `phos_store_ratio` itself) that produce marginally better biomass. The storage trap effect is real but secondary to the primary structural P supply deficit.

### Discovery 6: HPC Restart Architecture Limits Spinup-Protocol Testing

**Named:** "Fixed Restart P-State Barrier"

The A2MC architecture for HPC experiment cycles (Phase 5) branches experiments from a fixed spinup restart file generated from the base case (Case #86). Parameter changes tested in the transient simulation cannot retroactively improve the soil P pool state established during spinup. This means that parameters controlling P cycling during spinup (stoichiometry, microbial competition) — when changed only in the transient — cannot recover P pools that were depleted during the spinup itself.

This architectural feature explains why the 7 skip-testing iterations found correct directional signals in the Morris ensemble (where parameter changes affected the full simulation including spinup) but the 12 HPC transient experiments produced 0/6 targets (where the depleted P spinup state was fixed). The discovery has important implications for future experiment design: spinup protocol changes require Phase 0 redesign and full re-spinup, not targeted HPC experiments.
