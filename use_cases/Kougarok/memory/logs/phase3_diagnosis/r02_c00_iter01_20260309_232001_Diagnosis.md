## Likely Causes

1. Universal catastrophic P starvation: all three PFTs show P uptake/demand ratio of ~0.000, with total demand 358,121 g/m2/yr vs supply 0.67 g/m2/yr — a 530,000x imbalance that makes meaningful biomass accumulation impossible under ECA competition
2. PFT10 ECA competitive exclusion: PFT10 captures only 2.0% of total P uptake (0.013 g/m2/yr) while PFT7 dominates at 73.4%, leaving graminoid in a near-zero P state despite vmax_p_10 being sampled down to its minimum bound (5e-11)
3. L2FR amplification feedback: extreme l2fr_ini values (9.88 for PFT10, 18.31 for PFT9, both at upper bounds in Case 322) generate enormous fine root C pools which ECA interprets as enormous P demand, deepening the starvation rather than relieving it — a positive feedback loop
4. Parameter space misalignment: 46 of 162 parameters (28.4%) at sampling bounds in the best case, with 6 P-uptake parameters at extremes, confirming the Morris ensemble was not centered near the feasible solution region for this arctic P-limited system
5. PFT10 structural collapse from allometry: allom_d2bl1_10 at lower bound (0.019), allom_dbh_maxheight_10 at lower bound (0.191), and leaf_slatop_10 at lower bound (0.00853) together create an allometric regime that may prevent PFT10 from reaching functional canopy size, compounding the P limitation
6. vmax_p_10 at absolute minimum: Case 322 has vmax_p_10 = 5e-11 (at lower bound), which gives PFT10 virtually no competitive ability for P in ECA framework regardless of root biomass

## AI Reasoning and Deep Analysis

The diagnosis centers on a universal and catastrophic phosphorus limitation affecting all three PFTs (7, 9, 10) at Kougarok. The PFT summary confirms P limitation is the primary nutrient constraint for all PFTs, with uptake/demand ratios of effectively 0.00 across the board. The P mass balance reveals total P demand of 358,121 g/m2/yr against total uptake of only 0.67 g/m2/yr — a supply/demand ratio of ~1.9e-6, indicating the ECA competition framework is computing astronomically high demands relative to what soil can supply. This is not a moderate P limitation but a complete biochemical starvation of the CNP allocation system.

Causal chain: Extremely high P demand (driven by high vmax parameters and/or very high stoichiometric requirements) → near-zero supply/demand ratios → PID controller diverts all C allocation away from leaves toward root investment in an attempt to increase P uptake → leaf biomass collapses, especially for PFT9 and PFT10 → fine root biomass also collapses for PFT10 because even root production requires P for structural stoichiometry → targets missed by 78-99%.

The edge analysis confirms this mechanistically: for Case 322, vmax_p_10 is at its LOWER bound (5e-11), vmax_ptase_9 and vmax_ptase_10 are at LOWER bounds, while vmax_p_9 is at UPPER bound (5e-05) — yet even at max vmax_p_9, demand still overwhelms supply by orders of magnitude. The parameter ranges for vmax are clearly mismatched to the actual P supply rates at Kougarok.

Key finding: The P demand calculation uses fine-root carbon × vmax. PFT9 has l2fr_ini_9 = 18.31 (at upper bound) and PFT10 has l2fr_ini_10 = 9.88 (at upper bound), meaning these PFTs are allocating heavily to roots. With stoich_nitr_fineroot values and stoich_phos_fineroot values defining the stoichiometric P requirement per unit root C, and with such extreme l2fr values, the total P demand becomes impossibly large for the soil system to satisfy.

The test_l2fr_carbon_limitation hypothesis had confidence 0.60 but was marked 'Not Supported' — however, this test was looking at L2FR as a carbon limitation mechanism rather than as a P demand amplifier. The real mechanism is: extreme L2FR values (forced by the PID controller trying to acquire more P) generate enormous root biomass, which in ECA computes as enormous P demand (demand = fnrt_c × vmax), which further deepens the P starvation in a positive feedback loop.

PFT10 (Arctic graminoid) is most severely impacted: leaf = 1.1 vs target 82.7 (-98.7%), fineroot = 1.8 vs target 382.1 (-99.5%). This near-total collapse suggests either: (a) PFT10 is losing the ECA competition to PFT7 and PFT9 which dominate 73.4% and 24.6% of P uptake respectively, leaving only 2.0% for PFT10, or (b) PFT10's vmax_p_10 at lower bound (5e-11) is too low to compete even for available P, or (c) PFT10's allometry parameters (allom_d2bl1_10 at lower bound 0.019, allom_dbh_maxheight_10 at lower bound 0.191) are causing structural issues with canopy/crown area computation that prevent normal growth.

The most critical redesign needs are: (1) vmax_p parameters need to be re-evaluated against actual Kougarok soil P availability, (2) l2fr_ini bounds need to be constrained to ecologically plausible values for arctic shrubs/graminoids, and (3) allometry parameters for PFT10 need expansion.

## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT10: vmax_p_10) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** At absolute lower bound (5e-11) in best case, giving PFT10 near-zero P acquisition rate; ECA competition means PFT10 cannot compete against PFT7 (vmax_nh4_7=2.5e-4) for P

### fates_allom_l2fr (PFT9: l2fr_ini_9, PFT10: l2fr_ini_10) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** Both at upper bounds (18.31 and 9.88 respectively) in Case 322; extreme values amplify P demand calculation in ECA (demand = fnrt_c × vmax), creating an impossible-to-satisfy demand that forces allocation away from leaves

### fates_stoich_phos (PFT9 and PFT10 leaf and fineroot) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** Stoichiometric P requirements set too high relative to what Kougarok soil can supply; stoich_phos_leaf_9=0.00303 and stoich_phos_leaf_10=0.00299 define P demand per unit leaf C — if these are above realistic Kougarok plant tissue P concentrations, demand will always exceed supply

### fates_cnp_eca_vmax_ptase (PFT9: vmax_ptase_9, PFT10: vmax_ptase_10) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** Both at lower bound (5e-10) in Case 322; phosphatase production is minimal, reducing biochemical P mineralization pathway that could supplement direct uptake; BIOCHEM_PMIN is 0.533 g/m2/yr suggesting significant organic P pool that plants cannot access

### fates_allom_d2bl1 and fates_allom_dbh_maxheight (PFT10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** increase allom_d2bl1_10, allow allom_dbh_maxheight_10 to vary more freely
- **Issue:** allom_d2bl1_10 at lower bound (0.019) and allom_dbh_maxheight_10 at lower bound (0.191) may constrain PFT10 crown/leaf area allometry too severely, limiting maximum achievable leaf biomass regardless of C supply

### fates_cnp_pid_kp (PFT9: pid_kp_9, PFT10: pid_kp_10) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** investigate optimal value in 0.001-0.005 range
- **Issue:** PID proportional gain may be too low (or not optimally tuned) — if the controller is not responding strongly enough to P deficit, allocation correction is delayed; conversely if kp is too high, the controller may overshoot and create oscillations

### fates_cnp_turnover_nitr_retrans and fates_cnp_turnover_phos_retrans (all PFTs) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** These being at upper bounds CONFIRMS nutrient stress is real; the direction should be to fix nutrient supply rather than further increase retranslocation
- **Issue:** nitr_retrans_9=0.7 and nitr_retrans_10=0.7 (both at upper bounds); phos_retrans_7=0.9 (at upper bound) — high retranslocation means the model is already maximally recycling nutrients from senescing tissues, indicating the system is under severe nutrient stress and has activated all internal recycling mechanisms

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- PFT7 dominates P uptake at 73.4% share despite having its vmax_nh4_7 at upper bound (2.5e-4) — high vmax parameters in PFT7 systematically outcompete PFT9 and PFT10 in ECA framework, meaning improvements to PFT9/10 vmax alone may not help if PFT7 continues to monopolize available P
- Shared phosphatase parameters (fates_cnp_eca_vmax_ptase) interact across PFTs through the common soil P pool — increasing phosphatase for PFT10 also affects P availability for PFT7 and PFT9
- l2fr_ini values control both fine root biomass AND P demand simultaneously — a high l2fr for PFT9 (18.31) increases PFT9's P demand so dramatically that it may suppress PFT10 more than any PFT10-specific parameter can compensate
- phen_mindaysoff_7 = 148.6 days (upper bound) and phen_mindaysoff_10 = 148.6 days (upper bound): both PFT7 and PFT10 are in dormancy for maximum allowed days, potentially reducing growing season length below what is needed to achieve observed biomass targets
- Shared maintresp_nonleaf_baserate (1.97e-6) affects maintenance respiration costs for all non-leaf tissues across PFTs — if too high, it creates a C drain that compounds P-induced allocation failures
