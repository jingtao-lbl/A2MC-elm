## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT#10) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** MANDATORY RANGE REDESIGN: expand upper bound to 5e-03, new range [5e-09, 5e-03], logarithmic center 1e-06. Literature values for arctic graminoid P uptake (Chapin et al. 1993): 1e-07 to 1e-04 gP/gC/s.
- **Issue:** Current ensemble range [5e-11, 5e-05] is structurally insufficient. In Case #322, vmax_p_10 sits at its LOWER bound (5e-11) providing only 0.013 g P/m²/yr against demand of 15.5 g/m²/yr. Even at upper bound (5e-05), joint favorable cases show leaf10=0.002 g C/m² — still essentially zero. 0/4890 cases produce viable PFT10 biomass. The ensemble ceiling is 3 orders of magnitude below the required value.

### fates_allom_l2fr (PFT#9) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** RANGE CONTRACTION AND CENTER SHIFT: new bounds [0.5, 6.0], center at 2.5. The current upper bound of 18.31 should be reduced to 6.0 based on arctic shrub field data. The best case (#322) is at the upper extreme — reduction will shift C from roots to leaves, partially closing the PFT9_leaf gap (101.8 vs 124.7 g C/m²).
- **Issue:** l2fr_ini_9 = 18.31 in Case #322 (at upper bound). This creates biologically impossible root-to-leaf carbon ratio for Betula nana (arctic deciduous shrub). Observed field values: 1-4. The inflated root biomass drives PFT9 P demand to 165,550 g/m²/yr — 247x higher than arctic soil P supply. Skip-testing confirmed l2fr_reduction_beneficial=True across 3 cycles (corr=-0.257).

### fates_allom_l2fr (PFT#10) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** RANGE SHIFT: new bounds [1.0, 8.0], center at 4.0. Arctic graminoids with high root investments should use l2fr = 3-6 (matching field observations). The current upper bound of 9.88 creates excessive root demand without corresponding P supply.
- **Issue:** l2fr_ini_10 = 9.88 in Case #322 (at upper bound). For arctic graminoids (Eriophorum, Carex), typical L2FR is 2-8. At l2fr=9.88, root biomass severely inflates P demand for PFT10. Note: Case #1386 (which achieves PFT10 viability) likely has lower l2fr_ini_10 — the contrast between Case #322 (l2fr_10=9.88, PFT10_leaf=6.6) and Case #1386 (PFT10_leaf=37.0) may be partially explained by different l2fr_10 values.

### fates_cnp_eca_decompmicc (PFT#7) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** DECREASE to default range center: new upper bound 450 (from 600), proposed center 280 (default). The full range [140, 600] is appropriate but should be explored more uniformly — Case #322 has microb_bio_7 pinned at upper bound, which is mechanistically incorrect for Kougarok arctic soils.
- **Issue:** microb_bio_7 = 600 in Case #322 (at upper bound). This gives microbial competitors maximum advantage in ECA, causing PFT7 to capture 73.4% of total P uptake. Arctic tundra active layer microbial biomass is constrained by low temperatures — 600 g C/m³ is unrealistically high for permafrost-affected soils (typical: 100-400 g C/m³). Skip-testing showed low vs high microb_bio_7 provides 1.59x more froot7 biomass.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT#7 and PFT#9) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** COORDINATED RANGE EXPANSION: PFT7 new range [5e-08, 5e-03], PFT9 new range [5e-07, 5e-03]. The relative ratios vmax_p7:vmax_p9:vmax_p10 should be constrained to maintain biological realism (shrubs typically have higher per-gram uptake capacity than graminoids due to mycorrhizal associations).
- **Issue:** Must be expanded proportionally with PFT10 to maintain ECA competitive balance. In Case #322: vmax_p_7=2.86e-05 (near upper bound), vmax_p_9=5e-05 (at upper bound). If only PFT10 vmax_p is expanded, ECA competitive balance will shift further against PFT10 since larger PFT7/PFT9 plants will still dominate soil P acquisition.

### fates_cnp_phos_store_ratio (PFT#9) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** INCREASE: new range [2.0, 8.0] (currently [1.0, 5.0] with Case #322 at minimum). A value of 3-4 would provide P storage buffer while remaining physiologically realistic for Betula nana.
- **Issue:** phos_store_ratio_9 = 1.0 in Case #322 (at lower bound). Minimum P storage means absorbed P immediately cycles to organ demand with no buffer. P efflux = 0.43 g/m²/yr removes 64% of absorbed P — a significant loss pathway. Arctic shrubs store luxury P for spring leaf flush.

### fates_mort_hf_sm_threshold (PFT#10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** INCREASE: new lower bound 1e-07 (from 1e-08), new range [1e-07, 1e-06]. Arctic graminoids (Eriophorum, Carex) have aerenchyma and shallow roots that access surface water — they are drought-adapted and should have lower hydraulic failure sensitivity than shrubs.
- **Issue:** mort_hf_sm_threshold_10 = 1e-08 in Case #322 (at lower bound). Near-zero soil moisture threshold triggers maximum hydraulic failure mortality even during minor dry periods. This prevents PFT10 biomass accumulation even if P supply improves. Previously diagnosed as causing 92% of PFT10 mortality from hydraulic failure.

- ECA P Competition Conflict: PFT7's high vmax_nh4_7 (0.00025, upper bound in Case #322) and high microb_bio_7 (600, upper bound) combine to give PFT7 disproportionate P uptake (73.4% of total). This opposing error pattern for the SAME PFT suggests the PID controller is misallocating C — directing excess C to leaves (to restore N:C ratios depleted by P starvation) at the expense of roots. Any shared parameter fix (e.g., PID controller gains) must address this PFT7 internal conflict.
- l2fr Conflict Between PFT9 and PFT10: Both l2fr_ini_9 (18.31) and l2fr_ini_10 (9.88) are at upper bounds in Case #322. Reducing both simultaneously may create a situation where root biomass drops faster than leaf biomass adjusts, temporarily overshooting leaf targets while undershooting froot targets. Coordinated reduction with PID controller gains is required.
