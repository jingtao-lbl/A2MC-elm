## Likely Causes

1. PRIMARY: System-wide phosphorus starvation with P uptake/demand ratio of 0.000002. Total P uptake is 0.67 g/m²/yr against demand of 358,121 g/m²/yr. This is the root cause of ALL target failures.
2. SECONDARY: PFT9-PFT10 zero-sum P competition. PFT7 captures 73.4% of available P, PFT9 gets 24.6%, PFT10 only 2.0%. Improving one PFT's P access degrades the other's (Case #322 vs #1386 demonstrate this trade-off).
3. TERTIARY: PFT10 allometric constraints in Case #322 compound P starvation. allom_d2bl1_10=0.019 (lower bound), leaf_slatop_10=0.00853 (lower bound), allom_dbh_maxheight_10=0.192 (lower bound) minimize PFT10 capacity even when P is partially relieved.
4. QUATERNARY: PID controller instability under chronic P limitation. L2FR oscillations (CV=1.23 for PFT7 in Case #322) as the allocation controller futilely shifts carbon to roots seeking P that doesn't exist.
5. QUINARY: Phenology-mediated growing season limitation. phen_gddthresh_c is the strongest single correlate (r=-0.326) with PFT10 biomass, suggesting growing season length modulates the P starvation effect.

- **Direction:** increase to 1e-05 to 5e-05 (3-4 orders of magnitude within ensemble range [5e-10, 5e-04])
- **Issue:** At absolute lower bound (5e-10) in Case #322, despite vmax_p_9 being at upper bound (5e-05). PFT9 has exhausted the mineral P uptake pathway. Phosphatase is the only remaining lever to increase PFT9 P supply without competing with PFT10's mineral P pathway.

- **Issue:** At absolute lower bound (5e-10) in Case #322. Case #1386 has 2.1e-04. Phosphatase enables PFT10 to access the massive organic P pool (>1100 g P/m²), providing a parallel P supply pathway.

- **Issue:** At upper bound (0.002995) in Case #322, maximizing P demand per unit leaf C. This is the worst possible value under P starvation - every gram of leaf requires maximum P investment. ### fates_stoich_phos (PFT9, leaf organ) (Priority: 5)

- **Issue:** At 0.00303, near upper portion of range [0.002095, 0.004279]. Reducing P demand for PFT9 frees some P for PFT10 coexistence without requiring more total ecosystem P supply.

Case #1386 has 0.0377 (2x higher) with viable PFT10. Evidence: r=-0.081 with PFT10 leaf biomass (weak but correct direction given universal extinction).

- **Issue:** At lower bound (0.7) in Case #322. Higher retranslocation (→0.89) means 89% of leaf P is recycled vs 70%, reducing net P loss by 63% per turnover cycle. This is an internal recycling pathway that does NOT compete with other PFTs for soil P.

- **Issue:** Strongest single correlate with PFT10 biomass (r=-0.326). More negative values → higher GDD threshold → later leaf-on → shorter growing season. Cross-PFT: r=0.273 for PFT7, r=-0.421 for PFT9. Case #322 has -0.0121.

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- PFT9-PFT10 P competition is zero-sum: Case #322 (viable PFT9, dead PFT10) vs Case #1386 (dead PFT9, viable PFT10). The P pool is only 0.67 g/m²/yr total uptake, insufficient for both PFTs. Resolution requires EXPANDING total P supply (via phosphatase for both PFTs) rather than redistributing it.
- phen_gddthresh_c is shared: making it less negative benefits PFT9 (r=-0.421) and PFT10 (r=-0.296) but hurts PFT7 (r=0.273). PFT7 leaf is currently 32.5 vs target 24.55, so some PFT7 reduction is beneficial.
- PFT7 dominates P uptake (73.4% share) because it is evergreen and has persistent fine roots year-round. Any increase in total ecosystem P supply disproportionately benefits PFT7 first. PFT-specific phosphatase rates provide the only PFT-specific P supply lever.
- vmax_p_9 is already at upper bound (5e-05) in Case #322. Further PFT9 P supply enhancement is ONLY possible via the phosphatase pathway (vmax_ptase_9) or by reducing PFT9 P demand (stoich_phos). FLAG: vmax_p_9 upper bound expansion should be considered in next Phase 0 redesign.
