## Likely Causes

1. System-wide phosphorus starvation: P uptake/demand ratio is 0.000002 across all PFTs, with total ecosystem P demand of 358,121 g/m²/yr vs uptake of only 0.67 g/m²/yr. P is trapped in litter pools (>1100 g P/m² in Case 322, >3000 g P/m² in Case 1386) and cannot be mineralized fast enough.
2. PFT9-PFT10 competitive exclusion for phosphorus: PFT7 captures 73.4% of available P uptake, PFT9 gets 24.6%, and PFT10 only 2.0%. When PFT10 P uptake parameters are increased (Case 1386), PFT9 leaf biomass collapses from 101.8 to 3.8 gC/m², demonstrating zero-sum P competition.
3. PID controller instability under severe P limitation: L2FR dynamics show extreme oscillations (PFT7 mean L2FR=50.2, CV=1.23 in Case 322; PFT9 mean L2FR=38.8, CV=1.06 in Case 1386) as the allocation controller tries futilely to compensate for chronic P starvation by shifting allocation to roots.
4. Carbon starvation mortality cascade: Both cases show persistent C starvation mortality across all PFTs. In Case 322, PFT7 C starvation mortality reaches 400 gC/m²/yr during peak episodes. In Case 1386, mortality is lower (peak ~50 gC/m²/yr for PFT7) but chronic across PFT9 and PFT10.
5. PFT10 allometric constraints at lower bounds in Case 322: allom_d2bl1_10=0.019 (lower bound), leaf_slatop_10=0.00853 (lower bound), and allom_dbh_maxheight_10=0.192 (lower bound) all minimize PFT10's capacity to produce leaf area and grow, compounding the P limitation.

The root cause chain is: (1) Severe system-wide P limitation → (2) PFT competition for scarce P → (3) Winner-take-all dynamics where only one PFT group can access enough P → (4) PID controller drives extreme L2FR oscillations trying to compensate → (5) Carbon starvation mortality eliminates P-starved PFTs. The P mass balance confirms: total P demand is 358,121 g/m²/yr vs total P uptake of only 0.67 g/m²/yr — a supply/demand ratio of 0.000002. This is not a parameter tuning problem; it is a fundamental P supply bottleneck requiring coordinated intervention across multiple parameter categories simultaneously.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT10) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** increase dramatically — propose 5e-05 (upper bound of current range) as starting point for HPC experiments
- **Issue:** At absolute lower bound (5e-11) in Case 322. Even Case 1386 value (1.4e-05) only achieves 26% of PFT10 froot target. P uptake capacity is orders of magnitude too low.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT9) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** increase upper bound beyond 5e-05 — FLAG: NOT IN CURRENT ENSEMBLE range, consider expanding to 5e-04 in next Phase 0 redesign
- **Issue:** Already at upper bound (5e-05) in Case 322, yet PFT9 leaf is still -78.6% below target. The current upper bound is insufficient.

### fates_stoich_phos (PFT10, leaf organ) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** decrease to 0.0012-0.0015 (lower half of range [0.000921, 0.002995])
- **Issue:** At upper bound (0.002995) in Case 322, maximizing P demand per unit leaf. Halving this would double effective P supply for leaf growth.

### fates_stoich_phos (PFT9, leaf organ) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** decrease to 0.0022-0.0025 (lower third of range)
- **Issue:** At 0.00303 in Case 322, near the upper portion of range [0.002095, 0.004279]. High P demand exacerbates limitation.

### fates_cnp_turnover_phos_retrans (PFT10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** increase to 0.85-0.90 — FLAG: current upper bound is only 0.9, may need expansion
- **Issue:** At lower bound (0.7) in Case 322. Higher retranslocation reduces P loss during leaf senescence, partially compensating for low uptake.

### fates_allom_d2bl1 (PFT10) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** increase to 0.07-0.10 (default or above)
- **Issue:** At lower bound (0.019) in Case 322, giving PFT10 minimal leaf area. Case 1386 has 0.0377 (2x higher) and achieves much better PFT10 biomass.

### fates_leaf_slatop (PFT10) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** increase to 0.020-0.025 (within range [0.00853, 0.029])
- **Issue:** At lower bound (0.00853) in Case 322. Both cases have this at minimum, meaning extremely thick leaves with very low area per carbon invested. Typical graminoid SLA is 0.02-0.03.

### fates_cnp_eca_vmax_ptase (PFT9 and PFT10) (Priority: 8)
- **Direction:** increase to 5e-06 to 5e-05 (4-5 orders of magnitude within bounds [5e-10, 5e-04])
- **Issue:** Both at lower bound (5e-10) in Case 322. Massive P is trapped in litter pools (1100-3000 g P/m²). Phosphatase is the enzymatic pathway to mineralize this organic P.

### fates_phen_gddthresh_c (shared) (Priority: 9)
- **Direction:** set to less negative value (closer to -0.005) to extend PFT10 growing season
- **Issue:** Strongest single correlate with PFT10 biomass (r=-0.326). More negative values shorten growing season. Value of -0.0121 in Case 322 is mid-range.

### fates_frag_seed_decay_rate (PFT10) (Priority: 10)
- **Direction:** decrease to 0.10 (lower bound)
- **Issue:** Second strongest PFT10-specific correlate (r=-0.240). Case 322 value is 0.193, which is at the low end. Even lower values improve seed bank persistence.

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- CRITICAL PFT9-PFT10 P competition tradeoff: Case 322 (good PFT9, dead PFT10) vs Case 1386 (viable PFT10, dead PFT9 leaf) demonstrates competitive exclusion for scarce P. Any increase in PFT10 P uptake capacity directly reduces P available to PFT9. Resolution requires INCREASING TOTAL P SUPPLY to the system (via phosphatase, weathering, or reduced P demand) rather than redistributing existing supply.
- phen_gddthresh_c shared parameter conflict: More negative values help PFT7 (r=+0.273) but hurt PFT9 (r=-0.421) and PFT10 (r=-0.296). No single value can optimize all three PFTs simultaneously.
- PFT7 dominates P competition: PFT7 captures 73.4% of P uptake despite having similar vmax_p values, likely due to its larger fine root biomass providing more uptake surface. Improving PFT9 and PFT10 P uptake will reduce PFT7's share, potentially degrading PFT7 leaf (+32.2% error would worsen) and froot (-50% error might improve).
