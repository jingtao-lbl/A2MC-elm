## Likely Causes

1. PFT9 excessive leaf-to-fine-root ratio (l2fr_ini_9=18.31 at upper bound in Case #322) diverts almost all carbon to roots, starving leaf biomass — Case #3972 with l2fr_ini_9=5.24 achieves PFT9_leaf=85.2 vs Case #322's 26.6, proving l2fr is the dominant bottleneck for PFT9 leaf failure
2. PFT7 insufficient fine root investment (l2fr_ini_7=0.85 in Case #322 is leaf-biased) produces only 62 g C/m2 fineroot vs target 174 — the opposite problem from PFT9, requiring HIGHER l2fr to increase root biomass
3. PFT10 structural allometric collapse from triple lower-bound failure: allom_d2bl1_10=0.019, allom_dbh_maxheight_10=0.191, leaf_slatop_10=0.0085 all at sampling lower bounds, preventing graminoid canopy formation regardless of nutrient supply
4. Systemic P starvation (total demand 358,121 vs supply 0.67 g/m2/yr) sets a universal background limitation, but is itself driven by astronomically inflated ECA demand from extreme l2fr values for PFT7 (vmax_nh4_7=0.00025, microb_bio_7=600 both at upper bounds) and the stoichiometric requirements exceeding soil supply capacity
5. PFT10 ECA competitive exclusion with vmax_p_10=5e-11 (lower bound) vs PFT7 vmax_p_7=2.86e-5 (570,000x higher), restricting PFT10 to only 2% of total P uptake even though its absolute P demand (15.5 g/m2/yr) is manageable relative to soil supply (0.67 g/m2/yr) if not dominated by PFT7/9 demand
6. PID controller derivative gain at lower bound for PFT9 (pid_kd_9=0.01) removes damping on allocation oscillations, allowing the l2fr feedback loop to overshoot toward extreme root investment without correction

## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_allom_l2fr (PFT9: l2fr_ini_9) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** Case #322 has l2fr_ini_9=18.31 at upper bound, generating massive fine root investment that starves leaf allocation. Case #3972 with l2fr_ini_9=5.24 achieves PFT9_leaf=85.2 vs Case #322's 26.6 g/m2, demonstrating this is the primary lever for PFT9 leaf recovery. The Morris ensemble upper bound of 18.31 is ecologically unrealistic for deciduous arctic shrubs.

### fates_allom_l2fr (PFT7: l2fr_ini_7) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** Case #322 has l2fr_ini_7=0.85, which is highly leaf-biased. PFT7_fineroot=62 vs target 174 (-64%) while PFT7_leaf=21 vs target 24.6 (passing). Increasing l2fr for PFT7 will drive carbon toward roots and away from leaves — but since PFT7_leaf is at target, the leaf loss can be absorbed while gaining fineroot biomass.

### fates_allom_d2bl1 (PFT10: allom_d2bl1_10) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** Case #322 has allom_d2bl1_10=0.019 at absolute lower bound [0.019, 0.15]. This parameter controls leaf biomass per unit stem diameter — at the minimum, graminoids produce virtually no leaf biomass from any given plant size. This is likely the PRIMARY structural cause of PFT10_leaf=1.1 vs target 82.7.

### fates_allom_dbh_maxheight (PFT10: allom_dbh_maxheight_10) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** Case #322 has allom_dbh_maxheight_10=0.191 at lower bound [0.191, 0.520]. This controls the stem diameter at which PFT10 reaches maximum height — at 0.191 cm, graminoids are constrained to extremely small size before height growth stops, limiting crown area and total leaf area.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT10: vmax_p_10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** Case #322 has vmax_p_10=5e-11 at lower bound. Although PFT10's absolute P demand is only 15.5 g/m2/yr (manageable), it receives only 2% of P uptake (0.013 g/m2/yr) vs 73.4% for PFT7. With vmax_p_10 3 orders of magnitude below vmax_p_7 (2.86e-5), PFT10 cannot compete in ECA regardless of root biomass.

### fates_stoich_phos (PFT9 leaf: stoich_phos_leaf_9) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** decrease
- **Issue:** stoich_phos_leaf_9=0.00303 in Case #322. While Case #3972 achieves better PFT9_leaf with stoich_phos_leaf_9=0.00210 (at lower bound), the dominant fix is l2fr reduction. Lower P stoichiometry reduces per-unit-leaf P demand, allowing the same soil P supply to sustain more leaf biomass.

### fates_cnp_pid_kd (PFT9 and PFT10: pid_kd_9, pid_kd_10) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** Case #322 has pid_kd_9=0.01 and pid_kd_10=0.01, both at lower bound [0.01, 0.5]. With no derivative damping, the PID controller cannot prevent overshoot in allocation responses to nutrient limitation signals, allowing extreme l2fr values to persist. Case #1391 (which achieves PFT10_fineroot=205) has pid_kd_10=0.43.

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- L2FR DIRECTIONAL CONFLICT: PFT9 requires l2fr DECREASE (18.31 → ~4) while PFT7 requires l2fr INCREASE (0.85 → ~1.5-2.0). Since fates_allom_l2fr is a PFT-specific parameter, these can be adjusted independently — no conflict. However, optimizing both simultaneously in a single experiment will require careful bounds to avoid overshooting.
- P UPTAKE ECA COMPETITION: Increasing vmax_p_10 for PFT10 recovery will marginally reduce P uptake share for PFT7 (currently 73.4%) and PFT9 (24.6%). Given PFT7/9 already have near-zero supply/demand ratios, this redistribution will not measurably worsen their P status — both are already in maximum starvation. No actionable conflict.
- PHENOLOGY SHARED PARAMETER CONFLICT: phen_mindaysoff is shared across PFTs but phen_mindaysoff_7 and phen_mindaysoff_10 are both at upper bound (148.6 days). Longer dormancy reduces growing season for both simultaneously, but the effect direction (reducing dormancy would help both PFT7 and PFT10) is aligned — no conflict.
- PFT9 FINEROOT OVER-TARGET: Case #322 has PFT9_fineroot=223.8 vs target 187.4 (+19.4%) — slightly above target. Reducing l2fr_ini_9 will bring fineroot down toward target while leaf increases. This is actually a beneficial coordinated response, not a conflict.
