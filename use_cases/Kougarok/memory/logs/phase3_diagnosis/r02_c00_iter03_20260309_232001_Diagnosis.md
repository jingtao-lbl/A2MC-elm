## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_mort_hf_sm_threshold (mort_hf_sm_threshold_10) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** increase significantly toward midpoint of range
- **Issue:** PFT10 hydraulic failure mortality dominates at 92% of total deaths (mean rate 12.58). mort_hf_sm_threshold_10=1e-08 (absolute lower bound of sampling range [1e-08, 1.44e-06]) means essentially any soil moisture condition triggers hydraulic failure death. This is the primary NEW finding: PFT10 plants cannot survive regardless of carbon or nutrient status because they die from hydraulic failure at a soil moisture threshold 100x lower than the sampling range maximum. Even if all other parameters are corrected, PFT10 biomass cannot accumulate when hydraulic mortality removes 92% of cohorts.

### fates_stoich_phos (stoich_phos_leaf_7 and stoich_phos_fineroot_7) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** decrease leaf and fineroot P stoichiometry for PFT7
- **Issue:** PFT7 is the dominant P demander at 192,556 g/m2/yr (54% of total P demand of 358,121 g/m2/yr), driven by stoich_phos_leaf_7=0.00181 and stoich_phos_fineroot_7=0.00109 combined with massive root biomass. The stoichiometry effectively sets the minimum P required per unit biomass — reducing P stoichiometry for PFT7 reduces its P demand proportionally and relieves ECA competition pressure on PFT9 and PFT10. Current stoich_phos_leaf_7=0.00181 is in the lower half of the ensemble range [0.00103, 0.00285], but fineroot stoichiometry × high root biomass is the primary demand driver.

### fates_allom_l2fr (l2fr_ini_9) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** decrease substantially
- **Issue:** l2fr_ini_9=18.31 at absolute upper bound of sampling range [0.01, 18.31]. This extreme value routes ~95% of new carbon to fine roots, starving leaf allocation for PFT9. Cycle 2 confirmed negative correlation (r=-0.257) between l2fr_ini_9 and PFT9_leaf, though all absolute values are near-zero due to systemic P starvation masking. The mechanism is directionally correct and should be addressed simultaneously with P demand reduction so that leaf recovery can occur once P limitation is partially relieved.

### fates_allom_l2fr (l2fr_ini_7) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** assess carefully — l2fr mechanism may be secondary for PFT7
- **Issue:** l2fr_ini_7=0.85 is leaf-biased, producing PFT7_froot=62 vs target 174 g C/m2 (-64%). The leaf-to-fine-root ratio in FATES maps as: higher l2fr → more leaf per root. With l2fr_ini_7=0.85, the model is already somewhat root-biased, but PFT7_froot remains severely below target. Cycle 2 confirmed positive correlation (r=+0.015, weak but directionally correct). The weakness of the correlation suggests l2fr_ini_7 is not the dominant bottleneck for PFT7_froot — P starvation preventing fineroot growth despite carbon availability may be more important.

### fates_cnp_pid_kd (pid_kd_10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** pid_kd_10=0.01 at lower bound of range [0.01, 0.5]. Case #1391, which achieves the highest PFT10_froot in the ensemble (205 g/m2), has pid_kd_10=0.43. The derivative gain provides damping that prevents allocation oscillations and allows stable allocation states to form. With zero effective damping, any perturbation in nutrient status causes the PID controller to overshoot in root allocation, creating a positive feedback loop.

### fates_cnp_pid_kd (pid_kd_9) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** increase
- **Issue:** pid_kd_9=0.01 at lower bound of range [0.01, 0.5], same as PFT10. This removes derivative damping from PFT9 allocation, allowing the l2fr overshoot toward 18.31 even after initialization with a corrected l2fr_ini_9. Without damping, l2fr may re-diverge dynamically during simulation.

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- The l2fr_ini parameter creates a zero-sum conflict between PFT9 and PFT7: reducing l2fr_ini_9 (to rescue PFT9_leaf) competes with the need to maintain PFT9_fineroot. The currently-passing PFT9_fineroot target (223.8 vs 187.4) provides some buffer — l2fr_ini_9 can be reduced to ~5-6 before PFT9_froot drops below the tolerance band. However, this must be calculated precisely.
- P stoichiometry reductions for PFT7 (Priority 2) will shift ECA P competition dynamics. Reducing PFT7's per-unit P demand means PFT7 absorbs a smaller fraction of total soil P, theoretically allowing more P to flow to PFT9 and PFT10. However, this is a non-linear ECA interaction — the change in uptake fractions depends on all three PFTs' root biomasses and vmax values simultaneously.
- The mort_hf_sm_threshold_10 change (Priority 1) affects ONLY PFT10 mortality, so it should not directly conflict with PFT7 or PFT9 targets. However, if PFT10 biomass recovers substantially, it will increase P competition pressure on PFT7 and PFT9 through the ECA mechanism — this is an indirect cross-PFT conflict. Monitor PFT7 and PFT9 biomass after PFT10 mortality correction.
- PFT7's vmax_nh4_7=0.00025 and microb_bio_7=600 are both at upper bounds and are the primary drivers of PFT7's outsized P demand. These parameters are NOT in the active investigation list but represent a fundamental upper-bound issue — PFT7 is demanding astronomically more N and P than the soil can supply, blocking all other PFTs from accessing nutrients. If these are not also corrected, reducing stoichiometry alone may be insufficient to rescue the system.
