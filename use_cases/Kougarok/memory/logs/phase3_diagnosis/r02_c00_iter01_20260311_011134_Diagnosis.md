## Likely Causes

1. PRIMARY: Complete ecosystem P starvation — total P demand (358,121 g/m²/yr) exceeds supply (0.67 g/m²/yr) by factor of ~535,000, indicating the parameter space does not allow biologically realistic P uptake rates. The vmax_p parameters are the direct control: vmax_p_10 is at its lower bound (5e-11) in Case #322 while the ecosystem is P-starved.
2. SECONDARY: Parameter space too narrow for vmax_p — the current upper bound for vmax_p (5e-05 for PFT9, 5e-11 for PFT10 at lower bound) allows a 6-order-of-magnitude range but the best case still uses extreme values, confirming the feasible region likely lies outside or at the edges of the sampled space. The test_morris_bounds_impact was confirmed with 95% confidence.
3. TERTIARY: PID controller-driven maladaptive allocation — in response to P starvation, the PID controller redirects carbon allocation heavily toward roots (L2FR = 0.34 for PFT7, root-biased allocation), starving leaves of carbon even when GPP is adequate. This creates a positive feedback: P-starved plants invest in roots → leaves decline → GPP falls → C starvation.
4. QUATERNARY: PFT10 (graminoid) near-complete collapse driven by hydraulic failure (92% of mortality) — the extreme low biomass (leaf: 1.1 g C/m²) suggests this PFT may be functionally extinct in simulation, unable to establish or maintain canopy. The allom_d2bl1_10 and allom_dbh_maxheight_10 parameters are at their lower bounds in Case #322, indicating the allometric constraints may be too small for graminoids.
5. QUINARY: Excessive P efflux (0.43 g/m²/yr vs uptake 0.67 g/m²/yr = 64% efflux-to-uptake ratio) — nearly half of all absorbed P is lost via efflux, suggesting a broken P storage-to-organ allocation cycle. This may be driven by very low phos_store_ratio_9 (at lower bound: 1.0) combined with high phos_retrans values (PFT7 at upper bound: 0.9).

## AI Reasoning and Deep Analysis

The diagnosis centers on a severe, ecosystem-wide phosphorus (P) limitation that is preventing biomass accumulation across all three PFTs. The PFT diagnosis tools confirm P limitation in ALL three PFTs (uptake/demand ≈ 0.00 for PFT7, PFT9, and PFT10). The P mass balance analysis reveals a catastrophic supply-demand mismatch: total P demand is 358,121 g/m²/yr while total P uptake is only 0.67 g/m²/yr — a supply/demand ratio of ~0.000002. This is not a minor imbalance but a complete P starvation state. The P efflux pathway (0.43 g/m²/yr, 38% of outputs) further indicates that the plant P cycle is dysfunctional — nearly as much P is being lost via efflux as is being taken up, suggesting the storage-to-organ P allocation is broken or the P pool is being cycled inefficiently. The edge analysis of Case #322 confirms that 6 CNP_vmax parameters are at bounds (22 at lower, 24 at upper, totaling 28.4% of all 162 parameters), and the test_morris_bounds_impact hypothesis was confirmed with 95% confidence (9/9 key parameters at bounds). This means the current sampling range for critical P-uptake parameters (especially vmax_p) is preventing the model from exploring a biologically feasible P-uptake regime. Specifically: vmax_p_10 is at its lower bound (5e-11) while vmax_p_9 is at its upper bound (5e-05) — a 6-order-of-magnitude difference in the 'best' case, suggesting extreme PFT-specific tuning that is still insufficient. The mortality analysis reveals PFT10 is dominated by hydraulic failure (92%), PFT9 by carbon starvation (86%), and PFT7 by hydraulic failure (76%) — all consistent with severe nutrient stress causing growth failure that then triggers carbon depletion and hydraulic stress. The causal chain is: Insufficient P supply → P starvation → PID controller reallocates C to roots (low L2FR observed: 0.34 for PFT7) → leaves cannot grow → GPP collapses → carbon starvation → mortality cascade. PFT10 (arctic graminoid) is the most severely affected, with leaf biomass at 1.1 vs target 82.7 g C/m² (-98.7%) and fineroot at 1.8 vs 382.1 g C/m² (-99.5%), indicating near-complete collapse. The parameter ranges for vmax_p need to be expanded by at least 2 orders of magnitude upward, the km_p values may be constraining uptake kinetics, and the L2FR ratios are too root-biased due to P starvation feedback. ASCII conceptual model: [Insufficient vmax_p range] → [P supply << demand (ratio ~2e-6)] → [PID drives C to roots (low L2FR)] → [Leaf mass collapses] → [GPP falls] → [C starvation mortality PFT9 86%] + [Hydraulic failure PFT10 92%, PFT7 76%] → [PFT10 near-collapse, PFT9 severe underperformance]

## Parameter Recommendations

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT10) (Priority: 1)
- **Direction:** increase — expand range to [5e-11, 5e-03], shifting center to ~1e-08 (2 orders of magnitude above current default of 5e-10)
- **Issue:** vmax_p_10 is at its lower bound (5e-11) in Case #322, while PFT10 demand is 15.5 g/m²/yr vs uptake of 0.013 g/m²/yr. The current upper bound (5e-05) needs to be the new lower exploration boundary for PFT10.

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT9) (Priority: 2)
- **Direction:** increase upper bound to 5e-03, but also investigate whether stoich_phos or l2fr_ini_9 is creating unrealistic root biomass that inflates P demand
- **Issue:** vmax_p_9 is at its upper bound (5e-05) in Case #322, yet supply/demand ratio is still ~0.000001. PFT9 demands 165,550 g/m²/yr of P which is physically impossible — this signals a stoichiometry problem: froot biomass × vmax creates unrealistic demand.

### fates_allom_l2fr (PFT9, PFT10) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** decrease l2fr_ini_9 range to [1, 8] and l2fr_ini_10 range to [1, 6]. High L2FR with P starvation creates a death spiral (more roots → more P demand → more starvation → more roots).
- **Issue:** l2fr_ini_9 = 18.3 (at upper bound) creates massive root biomass that drives astronomical P demand. l2fr_ini_10 = 9.88 (at upper bound) similarly drives excessive root allocation. The root-biased state for PFT9 results in leaf biomass of only 26.6 vs target 124.7 g C/m².

### fates_cnp_vmax_p (PFT7) (Priority: 4)
- **Direction:** increase upper bound to 5e-04 for PFT7 vmax_p
- **Issue:** PFT7 P demand is 192,556 g/m²/yr vs uptake of 0.49 g/m²/yr — supply/demand ≈ 2.5e-6. The parameter vmax_p_7 is at its upper bound (2.86e-05) in Case #322. Despite being highest P acquirer (73.4% of ecosystem uptake), PFT7 is still severely P-limited.

### fates_cnp_phos_store_ratio (PFT9) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** increase — set range to [2.0, 8.0] for PFT9. Arctic shrubs maintain significant P stores for spring growth.
- **Issue:** phos_store_ratio_9 = 1.0 (at lower bound in Case #322). Minimum P storage capacity means any absorbed P immediately cycles back — contributing to the high efflux rate (38% of outputs). Low storage prevents the plant from maintaining a P buffer to support growth.

### fates_cnp_eca_km_p (all PFTs) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** decrease km_p for all PFTs — arctic mycorrhizal plants are adapted to very low P concentrations. Consider range [0.01, 0.10] (10x lower than current).
- **Issue:** km_p_7 = 0.121, km_p_9 = 0.136, km_p_10 = 0.064 — these Michaelis-Menten half-saturation constants determine how efficiently roots acquire P at low soil concentrations. Arctic soils have very low P concentrations; if km_p is too high relative to soil P, uptake is always in the linear (unsaturated) regime.

### fates_allom_d2bl1 (PFT10) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** This parameter may need to increase if graminoid allometry is being constrained. Consider range [0.05, 0.25] to allow more leaf mass per unit diameter.
- **Issue:** allom_d2bl1_10 = 0.019 (at lower bound), which sets the leaf biomass allometric coefficient. Arctic graminoids have a very different allometric relationship than default FATES PFTs — the lower bound may be physically correct for graminoids (no woody tissue), but combined with P starvation, produces near-zero leaf biomass.

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- ECA nutrient competition conflict: PFT7 dominates P uptake (73.4% share) due to highest vmax_nh4_7 (at upper bound: 0.00025) and large fineroot biomass. Increasing vmax_p_7 further will competitively suppress PFT9 and PFT10 uptake shares. A coordinated approach must ensure all three PFTs have proportional vmax_p increases.
- L2FR cross-PFT conflict: l2fr_ini_9 = 18.3 (upper bound) creates massive PFT9 root biomass that increases ECA competition for P, further suppressing PFT10 (graminoid) which has the smallest fineroot biomass (1.8 vs 382.1 target). Reducing l2fr_ini_9 would benefit PFT10 by reducing competitive exclusion.
- Stoichiometry-demand conflict: PFT9 stoich_nitr_leaf_9 = 0.058 (upper bound) and stoich_phos_leaf_9 = 0.003 (near upper bound) set the nutrient demand per unit leaf. Combined with extreme L2FR and high root biomass, these create astronomical nutrient demands that cannot be met by any realistic uptake rate.
- Phenology shared parameter conflict: phen_mindaysoff_7 and phen_mindaysoff_10 are both at upper bound (148.6 days), meaning both PFT7 (evergreen shrub) and PFT10 (graminoid) are spending maximum time dormant. This reduces the growing season available for P uptake and biomass accumulation, compounding the P limitation.
