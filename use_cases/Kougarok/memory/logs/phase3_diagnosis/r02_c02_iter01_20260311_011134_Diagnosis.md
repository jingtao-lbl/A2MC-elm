## Likely Causes

1. PRIMARY: vmax_p_10 ensemble ceiling constraint — the current upper bound (5e-05) is biologically insufficient for arctic graminoids. Case #1386 at 1.43e-05 achieves only partial PFT10 viability (leaf=37 gC/m²). Literature values for arctic graminoid P uptake kinetics are 1e-07 to 1e-04 gP/gC/s. Zero viable PFT10 cases (leaf>5) exist in 4890 simulations even at ensemble maximum.
2. PRIMARY: vmax_ptase_10 phosphatase bypass mechanism — Case #1386 has vmax_ptase_10 at 4.28e-05 (428,571x higher than Case #322's 5e-10). High phosphatase capacity enables PFT10 to access organic P pools bypassing ECA inorganic P competition, the key mechanism enabling partial PFT10 viability.
3. SECONDARY: ECA competition lock-in — PFT7 captures 73.4% of all P uptake due to high vmax_p_7 (at upper bound) and microb_bio_7=600 (upper bound). This leaves PFT10 (2.0% uptake share) chronically P-starved regardless of its own vmax_p settings within current bounds.
4. SECONDARY: PFT9-PFT10 zero-sum ECA conflict — cases achieving PFT9 viability systematically collapse PFT10 and vice versa. This mutual exclusivity arises from shared P pool competition where improving one PFT's kinetics starves the other. Resolving this requires coordinated redesign of relative vmax_p ratios across all three PFTs.
5. TERTIARY: l2fr_ini_9 at upper bound (18.31) creates biologically unrealistic PFT9 root biomass, inflating P demand by ~5x over realistic values and consuming P that would otherwise be available to PFT10 via ECA competition.
6. TERTIARY: PFT7 fine root biomass deficit (-64%) co-driven by P limitation and possibly leaf-biased allometry, with allom_agb2_7 at upper bound (0.851) and leaf_slatop_7 at upper bound (0.0172) in Case #322

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- CONFIRMED ZERO-SUM ECA CONFLICT: Cases achieving PFT9 viability (Case #322: PFT9_leaf=101.8) systematically collapse PFT10 (leaf=6.6), and vice versa (Case #1386: PFT10_leaf=37, PFT9_leaf=5.67). This is the dominant cross-PFT interaction. Root cause: all three PFTs compete for the same inorganic P pool via ECA. When any PFT has high vmax_p relative to others, it captures disproportionate P share (PFT7: 73.4%, PFT9: 24.6%, PFT10: 2.0% in Case #322). Solution requires COORDINATED vmax_p redesign maintaining specific inter-PFT ratios.
- ECA DOMINANCE HIERARCHY: PFT7 (evergreen shrub) dominates P competition in 4890/4890 simulations due to large vmax_p relative to PFT10. Even when PFT10 vmax_p approaches upper bound, ECA gives PFT7 competitive advantage through microb_bio_7=600. Reducing microb_bio_7 and expanding PFT10/PFT9 vmax_p simultaneously is required.
- PHOSPHATASE BYPASS VS DIRECT UPTAKE: The phosphatase pathway (vmax_ptase_10) provides PFT10 access to organic P independent of ECA inorganic competition. This is the discovered escape from the zero-sum conflict — PFT10 must rely on organic P via phosphatase while PFTs 7 and 9 compete for inorganic P. The new ensemble must sample high vmax_ptase_10 with proportionally lower vmax_p_10.
- L2FR-MEDIATED DEMAND CONFLICT: PFT9 with l2fr_ini_9=18.31 creates excessive root biomass that inflates P demand by ~5x, consuming ECA capacity that would otherwise benefit PFT10. This is an indirect cross-PFT conflict mediated through shared P pool dynamics.
- STOICHIOMETRY CASCADES: High stoich_nitr_leaf_9 (at upper bound 0.0582 in Case #322) and stoich_phos_leaf_10 (at upper bound 0.00299 in Case #322) increase nutrient demand for leaf construction, adding secondary P stress beyond the direct uptake limitation.
