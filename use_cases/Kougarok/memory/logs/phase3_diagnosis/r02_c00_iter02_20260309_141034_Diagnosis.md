## Likely Causes

1. System-wide phosphorus starvation: P uptake/demand ratio is 0.000002 across all PFTs. Total ecosystem P demand = 358,121 g/m²/yr vs total P uptake = 0.67 g/m²/yr. This is the primary bottleneck for all targets.
2. PFT9-PFT10 competitive exclusion for phosphorus: PFT7 captures 73.4% of available P, PFT9 gets 24.6%, PFT10 only 2.0%. When PFT10 P uptake is enhanced (Case 1386), PFT9 leaf collapses from 101.8 to 5.7 gC/m².
3. PFT10 allometric constraints at lower bounds: In Case #322, allom_d2bl1_10=0.019 (lower bound), leaf_slatop_10=0.00853 (lower bound), allom_dbh_maxheight_10=0.192 (lower bound) collectively minimize PFT10 capacity to produce leaf area and grow.
4. PID controller instability under chronic P limitation: L2FR dynamics show extreme oscillations (PFT7 mean L2FR=50.2, CV=1.23 in Case 322) as allocation controller futilely shifts carbon to roots seeking P.
5. Carbon starvation mortality cascade: Persistent C starvation mortality across all PFTs as excessive root allocation (driven by P starvation) depletes carbon reserves.

### fates_stoich_phos (PFT10, leaf organ) (Priority: 3)
- **Direction:** decrease to 0.0012-0.0015 (lower half of range [0.000921, 0.002995])
- **Issue:** At upper bound (0.002995) in Case #322, maximizing P demand per unit leaf C. Evidence ledger: proposed 3 times, 0 supports in ensemble testing. However, lack of support is because PFT10 is universally extinct in ensemble - the parameter effect cannot manifest without viable PFT10 populations. Mechanistically, reducing P demand is equivalent to increasing P supply but without competitive exclusion effects.

### fates_cnp_eca_vmax_ptase (PFT9 and PFT10) (Priority: 4)

- **Issue:** Both at lower bounds (5e-10) in Case #322. Massive P trapped in litter pools (>1100 g P/m²). Phosphatase is the enzymatic pathway to mineralize organic P. Case #1386 has vmax_ptase_10=2.1e-04, showing this pathway matters.

### fates_cnp_turnover_phos_retrans (PFT10) (Priority: 5)
- **Direction:** increase to 0.85-0.90 (near upper bound of 0.9)
- **Issue:** At lower bound (0.7) in Case #322. Evidence ledger: proposed 2 times, 0 supports in ensemble. Same caveat as stoich_phos - effect cannot manifest without viable PFT10. Higher retranslocation reduces net P loss per turnover cycle, a critical internal P recycling mechanism.

### fates_allom_d2bl1 (PFT10) (Priority: 6)
- **Direction:** increase to 0.06-0.08 (near default 0.07)
- **Issue:** At absolute lower bound (0.019) in Case #322. Evidence ledger: proposed once, not supported. Same ensemble-extinction caveat. Case #1386 has 0.0377 (2x higher) with much better PFT10 biomass. Controls leaf biomass per unit diameter - at 0.019, PFT10 plants have minimal photosynthetic capacity.

### fates_leaf_slatop (PFT10) (Priority: 7)
- **Direction:** increase to 0.020-0.025 (within range [0.00853, 0.029])
- **Issue:** At lower bound (0.00853) in Case #322. Evidence ledger: proposed once, not supported. SLA of 0.0085 m²/gC is unrealistically low for Arctic graminoids (typical 0.02-0.03). Low SLA means minimal light interception per unit leaf C.

### fates_phen_gddthresh_c (shared) (Priority: 8)

- **Issue:** Strongest single correlate with PFT10 biomass in ensemble (r=-0.326, p<1e-99). More negative values shorten growing season. Case #322 value=-0.0121 is mid-range.

### fates_frag_seed_decay_rate (PFT10) (Priority: 9)

- **Issue:** Second strongest PFT10-specific correlate (r=-0.240). Case #322 value=0.193, already at low end. Lower values improve seed bank persistence and recruitment.

### fates_stoich_phos (PFT9, leaf organ) (Priority: 10)

- **Issue:** At 0.00303 in Case #322, near upper portion of range [0.002095, 0.004279]. High P demand exacerbates PFT9 P limitation, contributing to -78.6% leaf biomass error.

## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- CRITICAL PFT9-PFT10 P competition tradeoff: Case #322 (good PFT9, dead PFT10) vs Case #1386 (viable PFT10, dead PFT9 leaf). Any increase in PFT10 P uptake capacity directly reduces P available to PFT9 through ECA competitive exclusion. Resolution requires EITHER expanding total P supply (via phosphatase for both PFTs) OR reducing P demand (via stoichiometry reduction for both PFTs) to make the P pool large enough for coexistence.
- Shared phenology parameter phen_gddthresh_c has OPPOSITE effects on PFT7 (r=+0.273) vs PFT9/PFT10 (r=-0.421, r=-0.296). Less negative values extend growing season for PFT9/PFT10 but may slightly reduce PFT7 biomass.
- PFT7 captures 73.4% of P uptake, acting as dominant competitor. Reducing PFT7 P dominance (e.g., by lowering its vmax values, currently at upper bounds) could free P for PFT9/PFT10 but would degrade PFT7 targets (already 32.2% over-predicted for leaf, -50% for froot).
