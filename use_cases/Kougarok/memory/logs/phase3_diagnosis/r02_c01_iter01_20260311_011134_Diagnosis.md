## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- FUNDAMENTAL ECA COMPETITION CONFLICT: In ECA mode, P uptake is determined by the ratio of each competitor's vmax×rootC to km×(total_demand). PFT7 currently captures 73.4% of P because its vmax_p (near upper bound, ~2.86e-05) combined with large root biomass (62.3 g C/m²) dominates the ECA denominator. Any fix for PFT10 P starvation that only increases vmax_p_10 without proportionally increasing all three PFTs' vmax will only partially help — PFT7 will remain dominant unless vmax_p_10 is increased by 3-4 orders of magnitude relative to vmax_p_7.
- L2FR CROSS-PFT CARBON CONFLICT: l2fr_ini_9=18.31 means PFT9 allocates 18x more carbon to fine roots than leaves. This creates an enormous root biomass sink that competes with PFT7 and PFT10 for P in the soil layer, while simultaneously starving PFT9 of leaf area for photosynthesis. The l2fr_ini_10=9.88 (also at upper bound) similarly inflates PFT10 root carbon allocation beyond what the PFT10 P supply can support.
- SHARED PHENOLOGY CONFLICT: phen_mindaysoff_7=148.58 and phen_mindaysoff_10=148.58 (both at upper bound) extend dormancy periods for both PFT7 (evergreen shrub) and PFT10 (graminoid). This shortens the growing season during which P uptake occurs, potentially worsening the P supply-demand imbalance by reducing total seasonal uptake capacity.
- RETRANSLOCATION UPPER BOUND CONFLICT: nitr_retrans_9=0.7, nitr_retrans_10=0.7 (both at upper bound), and phos_retrans_7=0.9 (upper bound) indicate these PFTs are recycling maximum possible N and P internally. While this is appropriate for nutrient-limited systems, it also means the model is already using internal recycling as a compensation mechanism, suggesting that soil uptake routes are genuinely failing and internal recycling alone is insufficient to meet demand.
