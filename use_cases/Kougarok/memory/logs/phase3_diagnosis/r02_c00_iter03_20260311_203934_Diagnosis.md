ROOT CAUSE 3 — PID CONTROLLER AMPLIFICATION TRAP (CONFIRMED, confidence=0.85): The PID controller (pid_kp_9=0.00357, pid_kp_10=0.00143 in Case #322) senses the P deficit and maximally redirects carbon to roots, which INCREASES fnrt_c, which INCREASES P demand, creating a positive feedback loop. 4. ROOT CAUSE 4 — P EFFLUX PARADOX (CONFIRMED, confidence=0.80): FATES_PEFFLUX=0.43 g/m²/yr = 38% of all P outputs despite catastrophic P limitation. In Case #322, phos_store_ratio_9=1.0 (at lower bound) minimizes P storage capacity, forcing any instantaneous P uptake to efflux rather than accumulate. This wastes 38% of the already-minuscule P supply (0.67 g/m²/yr gross uptake). Increasing phos_store_ratio reduces this wastage.
5. ROOT CAUSE 5 — PFT7 LEAF-BIASED ALLOCATION (CONFIRMED, confidence=0.75): l2fr_ini_7=0.85 with Warning 'Low allocation L2FR (0.34) - root-biased' from PFT7 diagnosis. The leaf_slatop_7=0.0172 (at upper bound) produces too much leaf area per unit carbon, and l2fr_ini_7=0.85 needs to be INCREASED to shift more carbon to roots.
6. REJECTED CAUSE — PFT10 HYDRAULIC FAILURE (Rejected by data, confidence=0.40): The hypothesis test (test_pft10_hydraulic_l2fr_joint_effect) showed only weak correlations between mort_hf_sm_threshold_10 and PFT10 leaf biomass (r=-0.082 linear, r=-0.108 log-scale) and unexpected positive correlation for mort_scalar_hydrfailure_10 (r=+0.042). ## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- CRITICAL CONFLICT — L2FR Direction Reversal Between PFTs: PFT7 needs HIGHER l2fr (observed root:leaf=7.1, current l2fr_ini_7=0.85 produces too many leaves), while PFT9 and PFT10 need LOWER l2fr (current values above collapse thresholds). These are PFT-specific parameters so no direct conflict, but the ensemble sampling must be redesigned to force them in opposite directions simultaneously.
- P COMPETITION CONFLICT — PFT7 Dominates P Uptake: PFT7 captures 73.4% of total P uptake (0.492 g/m²/yr) while PFT9 captures only 24.6% and PFT10 captures 2.0%. If PFT7 l2fr is increased (more fine roots), it will further increase PFT7 P demand and reduce P available to PFT9/PFT10. However, PFT7 P demand (192,556 g/m²/yr) is already effectively infinite — the competition is for 0.86 g/m²/yr total supply regardless.
- vmax_p RANGE CONFLICT — PFT9 Needs Higher Maximum, PFT10 Needs Higher Minimum: vmax_p_9 should be increased to >5e-5 (currently exhausted at upper bound) while vmax_p_10=5e-11 (at lower bound) needs to be increased to ~5e-8. These are in the SAME direction (both increase) but different magnitudes. No direct conflict, but the shared parameter range [5e-11, 5e-5] spans 6 orders of magnitude — sampling this range uniformly on log scale may be insufficient.
- PHENOLOGY CONFLICT — phen_mindaysoff at upper bound for both PFT7 and PFT10: Both PFT7 (phen_mindaysoff_7=148.6, at upper bound) and PFT10 (phen_mindaysoff_10=148.6, at upper bound) have maximum dormancy periods in Case #322. This reduces the growing season for both PFTs. If both need more growing season, this shared directional bias should be addressed in the redesign.
- STOICHIOMETRY SCALING CONFLICT — Reducing stoich_phos_fineroot for PFT9 will reduce P demand BUT also reduces the P requirement for root construction, which may alter how FATES allocates between P and C for root growth. This could interact with the PID controller response in non-obvious ways.
