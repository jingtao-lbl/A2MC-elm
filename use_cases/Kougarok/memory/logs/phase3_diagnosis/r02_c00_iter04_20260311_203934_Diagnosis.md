## Cross-PFT Conflicts

- SHARED P SUPPLY COMPETITION: All three PFTs compete for the same 0.86 g/m²/yr P supply. In Case #322, PFT7 captures 73.4% (0.49 g/m²/yr), PFT9 captures 24.6% (0.16 g/m²/yr), and PFT10 captures only 2.0% (0.013 g/m²/yr). Reducing PFT7 and PFT9 vmax_p will reallocate P supply to PFT10. This is a ZERO-SUM competition — PFT7 and PFT9 must be reduced to rescue PFT10.
- L2FR CROSS-PFT INTERACTION: From Cycle 3, l2fr_ini_9 positively correlates with PFT10 leaf biomass (r=+0.098) — when PFT9 collapses due to high L2FR, PFT10 benefits from reduced nutrient competition. This means reducing L2FR_9 to fix PFT9 will INCREASE nutrient competition with PFT10, potentially degrading PFT10 outcomes unless vmax_p_10 is simultaneously raised.
- PID CONTROLLER SHARED DYNAMICS: The PID controllers for all PFTs respond to the same soil P pool. If all PFTs simultaneously maximize root allocation (as they currently do when P-limited), the total P demand becomes catastrophically large. Constraining L2FR for all PFTs simultaneously and reducing vmax_p upper bounds will prevent the compounding of their individual demand explosions.
- SLA vs COMPETITION: Reducing leaf_slatop_7 reduces PFT7's light capture efficiency, potentially allowing more light to reach PFT9 and PFT10 understory plants. At Kougarok Arctic tundra, light competition is secondary to nutrient limitation, but improving PFT7's allocation efficiency may help all PFTs by reducing competition for carbon resources.
